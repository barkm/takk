# Roadmap — the TAKK practice app

Status of the app and the plan ahead, as ROADMAP.md holds them for the isolated sign verification work the app is built on (`src/isolated_sign_validation/`). Update this file when a step is finished, a decision is made, or the plan changes. Read the roadmap of the package you touch, both when a change crosses. See README.md for how to run the app.

The app (user, 2026-09-19): practising TAKK (tecken som alternativ och kompletterande kommunikation), from single signs to sentences. TAKK signs the key words of spoken Swedish with STS lexicon signs, in Swedish word order and closer to isolation than fluent STS, so a sentence is a known sequence of lexicon signs to find and score in order. What remains is splitting the recording into signs, possibly slightly blended forms, and errors adding up over a sentence's signs.

## The shape of the app

The user drew the app as a screen map on 2026-09-21, and it is what the steps below build towards. The sections are the map's; the layout around them was redesigned from scratch on 2026-09-23 (step 17), so the menu page and the camera above every page are gone and a rail of three sections stands beside the page instead:

- **Sök** (`/sök`) — finding signs and choosing which ones to learn. A search field ("Sök efter ord eller teman") and, under it, the camera to sign into produce a grid of signs, each tile the looping reference clip and the word. Pressing a tile adds the sign to the learner's vocabulary or takes it out again; the map's "Lägg till alla" above the results is gone (step 17).
- **Träna** (`/träna/ord`, `/träna/meningar`) — two halves of one section, switched between at the top of both: **Ord**, a word at a time with the lexicon's reference clip and the camera beside it, looping to the next word, and **Meningar**, a connected text read line by line over the words already learned. The map called them Nya ord and Repetera; step 16 renamed both. The camera belongs to this section and is the column beside what is being signed.
- **Tecken** (`/tecken`) — the learner's own signs: how many are chosen, practised and due now, a bar chart of how they are spread over the repetition schedule (nya, imorgon, 3 dagar, 7 dagar, 21 dagar), a line chart of signs met over time, the words themselves with the interval each has reached, and "Nollställ".

The first section is named for what the learner does there and the third for what it holds (user, 2026-09-21). The two were briefly the other way round, as "Tecken" and "Framsteg"; "Tecken" belongs to the learner's own signs, and the way in is a search.

## Steps

Steps 1 to 7 are built and in use. Steps 8 to 14 are the blueprint above and are the work ahead, in the order they are numbered: each one is useful on its own, and the state the app keeps grows only when a step needs it. Throughout, the app's numbers rest on the self-recorded Swedish clips of step 10 of ROADMAP.md, so they grow with that set: more signs and signers before any result from the app means much.

1. **Live landmarks in the browser** (`frontend/src/lib/tracking.ts`, `landmarks.ts`): the browser tracks the pose with the MediaPipe Tasks HolisticLandmarker on the GPU delegate, fully live (user, 2026-09-19) — VIDEO mode on every camera frame it keeps up with, skipping the frames that arrive while it is busy, drawn over the camera image, with the rate it reaches shown (a warning below 20 fps). A recording keeps each result with its capture time and is resampled to 30 fps, the latest tracked frame at each step, as the collection app's transcode picks frames. — done. Open: the real frame rate on the user's GPU, and whether results hold up when frames are skipped.

2. **Scoring an attempt** (`takk/practice.py`, `POST /api/attempt`): the server checks the recording, prepares it (mirrored by the attempt's dominant hand, or the user's stated handedness for two-handed attempts), embeds it with `iv14_h384_e20` and accepts when the mean cosine similarity to the sign's lexicon clips reaches the threshold (see decisions); it also returns the closest sign of the whole lexicon, which step 10 needs. The lexicon's embeddings are cached next to the run and take about 45 s to build at startup. Nothing is stored, there are no accounts and no user state on the server. — done, checked end to end in headless Chrome (see findings).

3. **Splitting a recording by the words that were spoken** (`takk/speech.py`): TAKK is spoken while signed, so the words are known and nothing is recognized (user, 2026-09-19). The browser records the microphone alongside the landmarks and `torchaudio.functional.forced_align` over `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish` times the key words in the audio, cutting halfway from one word's end to the next word's start, with a wildcard token between and around them absorbing the rest of the sentence so the signer speaks naturally. The outer edges sit `PRE_ROLL` (0.5 s) before the first word and `POST_ROLL` (0.4 s) after the last, so what is recorded before the signer begins and after they finish is not scored as part of the first and last signs. Splitting at the rests instead was built first and removed (see findings). Each word carries its alignment score and an attempt whose words were not heard is refused, or scored as a miss when the caller passes `unheard_is_miss` — the story does, since there the recording is not worth remaking. — done; the user confirmed in their own browser that a spoken sentence scores sign by sign, the same sentence signed in silence is refused, and a word said but not signed is a miss with a verdict of its own (2026-09-21).

4. **The recording begins and ends with the voice** (user, 2026-09-21), so nothing is pressed: `frontend/src/lib/voice.ts` reads the loudness of the newest 21 ms from an `AnalyserNode` on the microphone that is already open, fifty times a second, and `Recorder.svelte` arms a recording when the card appears, starts the attempt after three readings above the threshold and ends it after twenty-four under it. The quiet room is measured for 0.7 s first, since absolute levels mean nothing across microphones, and the threshold is three times that floor; a reading has to fall to six tenths of the threshold to count as quiet, so a soft last syllable does not cut the attempt short. Waiting armed for ten seconds throws the recording away and starts a new one, so a long wait is never sent. The reading is deliberately crude and that is the design: a window opened by a door has no spoken words in it, so alignment scores them under `MIN_WORD_SCORE` and the attempt is refused — a false start costs one window and never a wrong verdict, which is what pays for having no voice detector in the browser. Recording runs from when the card appears rather than from the first word, because the hands rise before the voice. — done, practised with in the user's own browser (2026-09-21). Every number is a knob and none has been measured.

5. **A SvelteKit app in Swedish** (`frontend/`, see the decisions): the camera, the landmarker loop, the common landmark layout and the API calls live in typed modules (`src/lib/`) that the components only render. Every string the learner reads is hardcoded Swedish, with no translation framework, because the app is for practising TAKK and has no other audience; the reasons a recording cannot be used come from `checks.py` in the verification package, which the English collection app shares, so the wording is a `notes` argument each app passes. `takk` is the API alone and never serves the page. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test`, `npm run build` and `uv run pytest`, and in the user's own browser (2026-09-21). Open: CORS on the API (`allow_origin`), needed the first time the page is served from another origin than the API.

6. **Progress and spaced repetition in the browser** (`frontend/src/lib/progress.ts`): per sign a Leitner box and a next-due date in `localStorage`. The four boxes are due again after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days (user, 2026-09-21), the whole schedule being the `DAYS` array. A miss drops a sign to the first box at once; an accept promotes it only if it was due (see decisions). `seen` records when a sign was last practised and `first` when it was met, which is what the charts of step 14 need. The card is stored beside the box — its word and its lexicon entry — because a box belongs to a sign class and a class has many words ("svart" showed up as "Oden" until the word practised was the one kept), and because practising a word again should look nothing up (user, 2026-09-21). — done, unit tested (`progress.test.ts`). Open: export and import of the store for a second device, and a recording that could not be used is currently neither an answer nor a repetition.

7. **Nya ord and Sagan**, the two modes the app has (user, 2026-09-21): new words are taught on cards (`frontend/src/routes/+page.svelte`) with the lexicon clip and the lexicon's own description of the sign's form (`GET /api/form/{entry}`, on all but 30 of the entries with a video), and everything else is repetition inside one Swedish story (`takk/story.py`, `POST /api/story`, `frontend/src/routes/saga/+page.svelte`) written over the words the learner already knows. A card is one word and nothing else, since the word is being taught rather than used, and the verdict says only "Rätt" or "Fel", named by the word that was asked for rather than the sign that scores it. Nothing is pressed between cards: a word signed right is recorded and the next card comes at once, a word missed or a recording that could not be used is simply armed again with the clip still on screen. The story's words are drawn without replacement and weighted by `1 / DAYS[box - 1]`, so a word in the first box is twenty-one times as likely as one in the last — that weight is the rate the ordinary schedule would meet the word at, so a story spends its words on the weakest ones. The daily pass, the review pass and free practice were all removed along the way; what they established is in the decisions and findings below. — done as far as it goes; the story is a prototype and step 13 rebuilds its page.

8. **Menyn** (`frontend/src/routes/+page.svelte`): the sections of the blueprint and nothing else, in place of the nav bar that used to sit above every page. Every page but the menu carries one way back (user, 2026-09-21), so nothing depends on the browser's own back button; it goes to the section above rather than to wherever the learner came from, since the map is a tree and a page's parent is its path without the last segment.

    **The menu asks which hand the learner signs with, once** (user, 2026-09-21), and `frontend/src/lib/hand.ts` keeps the answer in `localStorage`. Every recording is mirrored by it, so both camera screens used to carry a radio for something that never changes between attempts; they now read the stored answer and show nothing. It can be changed again under Tecken, beside "Nollställ", which is the one place the learner's own settings live. The pages moved to where the map puts them — the Nya ord cards to `/träna/nya`, the story from `/saga` to `/träna/repetera`, and `/träna` is their hub — and the third section stayed where it was. The pack picker is still on the Nya ord page and goes in step 9, which is when the app has another source of words; leaving it until then keeps the app usable between the steps. **Träna is only its two links for now**: the camera preview and the framing feedback the map draws on it belong to steps 11 and 12, which is also when the camera starts opening once for both modes. Sök is missing from the menu until step 9 makes it something to open. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build` (the routes with "ä" in them build and serve as `/tr%C3%A4na`). Not tried in a browser yet.

9. **Sök: choosing the vocabulary by searching the lexicon** (user, 2026-09-21). The search field takes a word or a theme and returns a list of signs, each row a checkbox and a video preview, with "Lägg till alla" above it; ticking adds the sign to the learner's vocabulary. This **replaces the packs** as the way vocabulary grows: `takk/packs.json`, `GET /api/packs`, the pack picker and `chosenWords`, `loadChosen` and `saveChosen` go, and `known` and `boxes` read the progress store alone. The lexicon's categories stay, as what a theme search matches: typing "mat" returns Mat och dryck ordered by `lexicon_hits` (see findings), so the everyday words come first. The one piece of new state is a sign that has been chosen but never practised — "nya" in the charts of step 14 — since a sign used to enter the store only when it was first accepted; `add` writes a box-0 entry with the word, the entry and the time it was picked, `fresh` is what Nya ord teaches from, and `record` is unchanged. Box 0 has no due date, and `known` skips it, so a sign is never repeated before it has been taught. Unticking a sign removes it again, unless it has been practised: its checkbox is then locked, since the tick is not what holds the learning. (Step 17 lifted that lock: a picked sign can always be unpicked, and unpicking drops its box.) A result's clip plays by itself, muted and looping, rather than waiting to be pressed, since the point of the row is to see the sign.

    **The store has no backwards compatibility** (user, 2026-09-21: "you don't have to assume that we should store any old word state. Let's start clean"). `Learned` now requires the box, the due date, the time it was picked, the word and the lexicon entry, and the fallbacks that stood in for a record written by an older version are gone — a sign the search did not name is named after the sign itself when it is picked, so the store always holds the word to show. A store written before this change is cleared with "Nollställ". — done, checked with `uv run pytest`, `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`; the search is unit tested against a small crawl (`tests/takk/test_vocabulary.py`) and the boxes against the new box 0 (`progress.test.ts`). Not tried in a browser yet, and the search has not been run against the real 21,680-entry crawl.

    Nya ord moved to the picked words in the same step rather than in step 12, because removing the packs leaves it with no other source. It takes the five longest waiting and says to go to Tecken when there are none.

10. **Sök med tecken** (`POST /api/search`): the camera records landmarks only — no microphone, because this is a lookup and not an attempt — and the server answers with the `CLOSEST` (20) lexicon signs nearest by cosine similarity, into the same rows the text search fills. The ranking is the one `judge` already computes over the whole glossary, and the recording is checked and prepared exactly as an attempt is, mirrored by the dominant hand; a recording the checks refuse answers with no rows and the reason. A sign is offered under the word it is best known by (`Index.by_sign`, the most counted word of that form), so a row from the camera is the same shape as a row from the field. — done, checked with `uv run pytest`, `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`; the endpoint is tested by ranking two signs and by a recording with no hands in it. Not tried in a browser yet.

    The camera is **a second state of the Sök page** rather than a page of its own, though the map draws it as one: the two searches fill the same list, so a route would have to hand the rows back to it. The map's flow is what is kept — field or camera, then the rows. The recording is started and stopped by hand ("Starta"/"Stopp"), unlike an attempt, which the voice begins and ends: there is nothing being said to begin it with.

    **The recording stays in the camera's own place** (user, 2026-09-21), as the searched word stays in the field: the tracked landmarks of the lookup replay on a loop over the live picture, in the same box and at the same size, with the rows under it. A search never switches the page back to the field either — the learner chose the camera, so it stays until they press "Sök med ord". Only the landmarks are kept and replayed, never the camera's picture, which is also all that was sent; `draw` takes a size rather than the video element it used to read one from. A word search clears the replay, since the rows are then an answer to the word, and so does the next recording.

    **The camera closes when the component goes away** (found while sharing it, 2026-09-22). `Tracker` had no teardown at all: leaving a page left its `MediaStream` tracks open, so the camera light stayed on and the next page opened a second stream beside it. `Tracker.stop()` now stops every track, drops the video's source, closes the audio context and the landmarker, and lets the frame loop end; the component calls it when it unmounts.

    Once opened, the camera stays in the page and is only hidden (user, 2026-09-21: the second lookup showed a black picture). `Tracker` holds the video element it was started with and runs its loop on that element's `requestVideoFrameCallback`, so leaving the camera unmounted it and left the tracker driving a detached element while the remounted one had no stream. The same applies wherever the camera is shown and hidden, which step 12 does for both practice modes.

11. **Framing feedback under the camera** (`frontend/src/lib/framing.ts`): both camera screens say how the signer sits, read five times a second off the frame being tracked right now, so the framing is fixed before a recording is spent on it. One frame, a handful of distances and no history — `checks.py` still has the last word on a finished recording. It asks, in this order, for both shoulders, for a distance that keeps the shoulders between `NARROW` (0.16) and `WIDE` (0.55) of the frame width, for the signer to be within `OFF_CENTRE` (0.18) of the middle, and for `HEADROOM` (0.08) of the frame above the head, where a good many signs are made. Every number is a guess read off the lexicon's own framing and every one is a knob. **It is silent when the framing is right** (user, 2026-09-21): `framing` returns the empty string, and what is wrong is shown over the bottom of the picture it is about rather than as a line under it — unmirrored, since the view itself is flipped like a mirror. **It says nothing about the hands** (user, 2026-09-22). Asking for them while recording meant complaining about the pause the moment a sign ended. Holding a complaint back until it had lasted a second was tried first and removed with the rule it existed for: the framing itself — where the signer sits — does not flicker, so nothing else needed the wait. Whether a recording had hands in it is the server's word, in `checks.py`, after the fact. — done, unit tested on synthetic frames (`framing.test.ts`). Not tried in a browser yet.

    **The frame rate is no longer shown** (user, 2026-09-21), and with it went `Tracker`'s `onRate` callback, its `delegate` and the warning below 20 fps. The framing is what a learner can act on; the rate was a developer's number. The open question of step 1 — what the rate actually is on this GPU — now needs a measurement rather than a glance at the page.

12. **The camera is the app's own picture** (`frontend/src/routes/+layout.svelte`): it sits above every page, the menu included, with its framing feedback under it. The camera belongs to the **root layout** (user, 2026-09-22), which SvelteKit keeps mounted for the whole session, so it opens once and the element the tracker draws on is never taken out from under it — which is what black-screened the sign search before it was hidden rather than unmounted. It is above the three blocks of the menu, not behind them (user, 2026-09-22). The reason is a visual one rather than a saving: every screen then looks like the same app, and a tracked skeleton that moves with the learner is something to sign at. That it also costs one camera start and one landmarker load for the session, instead of one per section, is a side effect — caching the landmarker across mounts and a `Cache-Control` on the 14 MB `/api/model` were the alternatives, and neither is needed now. It changes the map, which gave the menu three sections and nothing else. `Camera` (`src/lib/camera.svelte.ts`) is what the layout puts in context: the tracker, the video element, the lexicon, the framing note, the overlay and whether a recording is running. The picture is `Camera.svelte`: it owns the video, the canvas the landmarks are drawn on, the startup, the framing loop and the note over the bottom of the picture, and a page that wants something in the picture — Sök's replay of the sign it searched with — hands it in as `camera.overlay`, a snippet, since the picture is no longer inside the page. The lexicon is fetched once, by the root layout, and the lines between the landmarks reach the tracker through `Tracker.edges` when it arrives, so the camera starts without waiting for the server. Which hand the learner signs with is no longer held by the camera but read from `hand.ts` when a recording is sent: the camera now starts before the menu has asked, and the answer can be changed under Tecken while it runs. `Recorder.svelte` is only the recording now — the voice, the timer and the scoring — and no longer opens a camera, holds a lexicon or draws a picture; the handedness radio moved to the layout, where it belongs to the camera rather than to an attempt. **The space for the picture is reserved while it starts** (user, 2026-09-22): the view keeps the camera's own 16/9 shape from the first frame of the page, the feed and the landmark canvas stay hidden until the tracker is ready — the camera opens before the landmark model finishes loading, and showing the raw feed in between was a black box of the wrong shape — and "Startar kameran …" stands in the middle of it until then, or the reason the camera could not open. On Sök the camera also sat in a wrapper that took its own width from the picture, so it grew from the video's size to the page's the moment the stream arrived; that wrapper is gone with the page's own camera. The lexicon's clips are held at their own 4/3 shape for the same reason, in the Sök results and on the Nya ord card. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. Not tried in a browser yet.

    **Nya ord shows its clip under the camera rather than beside it**, which the map draws side by side. The camera is one element above the page, and putting the page's content beside it would mean the layout laying out what it does not know. It is a CSS change in the layout if it turns out to matter.

13. **The story is read line by line** (user, 2026-09-21): `POST /api/story` writes `LINES` (6) lines in one request, and the page shows a window of them — `CONTEXT` (1) line above and one below in grey, only the line to sign now solid, with its signed words marked and coloured by their verdicts. There is no end screen and no summary: when the learner reaches the last written line the next six are asked for and appended, so the story goes on for as long as they keep signing (**superseded**: step 17 ended the story at its last line, and step 19 replaced the contract described below). A writer that failed is not asked again by itself, since a dead server would otherwise be asked forever; the page offers "Skriv fortsättningen" instead. `MOST_WORDS` (3 signed words per line) and the contract that each word appears exactly once and exactly as written stay as they are, and the verdicts are kept per line so an earlier line keeps its colours as it greys out. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. Not tried in a browser yet; `LINES` and `CONTEXT` are guesses and are knobs.

    **The mode is not called anything, and explains nothing** (user, 2026-09-21: "I don't want to call it 'saga' anywhere, this is just how you repeat words"). "Sagan" is gone from every page, and so is the paragraph that told the learner a text would be written over their words and read aloud — the page is a button, and then the lines. The English names inside the code (`takk/story.py`, `POST /api/story`) are not learner-facing and stay.

    Each request draws its words afresh from `known`, so the second six lines lean on whatever the first six left weakest. The model sees only the words of the request it is writing, so a story is continuous in the reading rather than in the telling — whether that shows is the first thing to look at in a browser.

14. **Tecken as charts** (`frontend/src/routes/tecken/+page.svelte`): a bar chart of the vocabulary over the schedule and a line chart of signs met over time, then "Nollställ", and nothing else. The bars are one per state, box 0 included (user, 2026-09-21) — nya, imorgon, 3 dagar, 7 dagar, 21 dagar — with the count written over each. The line is the cumulative number of signs with a `first` date, which is what "met" means: a sign picked and not yet practised has no such day and is not counted. Its axis runs from the earliest of those days to now, with both ends written out ("3 sep." and "idag", user 2026-09-21) — unlabelled, a week and a year looked the same. Both are read from the existing store, so no history is written, and both are inline SVG — one bar series and one line, each a single series, so there is no categorical palette to get wrong and no charting dependency to carry. The bars use the page's accent and the line its "right" green, the axes are a single recessive rule, and the counts are written directly rather than hovered for. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`; the geometry was checked by arithmetic rather than by looking at it, since the machine is headless. Not seen in a browser yet.

    **The table of every practised sign is gone**, with the counts per box it sat under: the map has a box for the charts and the reset and none for a table. It was where the spaced repetition was inspected while it was being built, so it is the one thing to bring back — behind a fold — if reading the boxes by hand turns out to be needed again.

15. **Later**, in the order they are likely to matter: a **conversation with an LLM** the learner answers by speaking and signing, corrected turn by turn, with the missed signs brought back in later turns, which is spaced repetition inside a conversation (user, 2026-09-20); **free signing**, where the learner speaks and signs whatever they like and the app reports which key words were signed — the speech model is a CTC model, so decoding its emissions gives the transcript and the content words of it that exist in the lexicon become the expected signs, after which the existing alignment and scoring run unchanged; and **state on the server**, accounts and a database, when the browser's own storage actually gets in the way (a second device, or more than one learner) and not before. — planned

16. **Ord teaches the words that are wrong, not only the words that are new** (user, 2026-09-22: "I need some way of repeating individual words that I'm getting wrong"). A missed sign already falls to the first box and is drawn twenty-one times as often in Meningar, which is the spread-out repetition; what was missing is the immediate one, with the clip on screen. That is what the teaching mode already is, so it teaches missed words too rather than the app growing a third mode: `toLearn` (the former `fresh`) returns the signs missed since they were last taught, then the ones picked and never practised, each group longest waiting first. The missed ones come first of all because a vocabulary picked in one sitting would otherwise bury today's mistake under a hundred words that have been waiting since. The one piece of new state is `missed`, the time of the last miss, which `record` writes on a miss and deletes on the next correct answer — a missed word and a word learned and fallen due both sit in the first box, so the box alone cannot tell them apart. Drilling a missed word cannot inflate its box: it is due tomorrow, so an immediate retry is an early answer and keeps both box and date (see the decisions).

    **The modes are named Ord and Meningar** (user, 2026-09-22), and the routes with them. "Nya ord" became untrue the moment the mode taught missed words as well, and "Repetera" named the wrong half — both modes are repetition, and what actually separates them is the isolated word with its clip in front of you from your own words in running sentences. One word each, and neither explains how the mode works, as the blueprint decision asks. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test`, `npm run build` and `uv run pytest`; `toLearn` and the new `missed` field are unit tested (`progress.test.ts`). Not tried in a browser yet.

17. **The interface, redesigned from scratch** (user, 2026-09-23: "I don't really like the current UI. Help me redesign it from scratch. No need to take any of our previous decisions into account."). The features are the same ones — spaced repetition, a way to find words, word practice, sentence practice and an overview — and nothing under `frontend/src/lib/` changed: the store, the tracker, the recorder and the API client are untouched, and so is the API itself. What changed is everything the learner sees, and it is a branch of its own (`worktree-ui-redesign`) so that it can be compared with main.

    **A rail of sections instead of a tree.** The three sections stand in a rail beside the page on a desktop window and as a bar along the bottom on a phone, so every page is one click from every other one. The menu page, the `/träna` hub and the "Tillbaka" link of step 8 are gone with it: nothing is behind anything any more, and `/` redirects to `/träna/ord`, the app opening on the practice it is opened to do. Ord and Meningar are a switch at the top of Träna rather than two destinations.

    **Dark, with one accent.** Near-black surfaces, a single blue accent, green and red kept for verdicts alone, and the system sans in place of the monospace the blueprint was drawn in. What is bright on the screen is the camera picture and the lexicon clips, which is what the learner looks at. The pastel blocks that told the sections apart by colour are gone; the rail tells them apart by position.

    **The camera only where it is used** (user, 2026-09-23), which reverses step 12: it is mounted by the Träna section and by Sök while a search by signing is running, so a page of results and the overview cost no camera and no landmarker. Träna mounts it in its layout rather than in either mode, so it still opens once and survives the switch between Ord and Meningar, and the hand is asked for there, before the first recording, instead of on the menu that no longer exists.

    **Desktop first** (user, 2026-09-23): the work and the camera side by side in two columns, collapsing to one column with the picture on top under 860 px, and the rail becoming the bottom bar under 720 px.

    **Meningar scrolls** (user, 2026-09-24): the whole story so far stands in the page inside a box that fades out at both ends, and the line being signed is scrolled to the middle of it as it comes up, so the lines already signed stay above with the colours their words were given. Showing one line above and one below the current one, which the redesign inherited from step 13, is gone. The scroll is smooth unless the browser asks for reduced motion.

    **A story ends** (user, 2026-09-24). Signing its last line puts the page back at its start card, and the next story is written when the learner presses "Börja" again. The story used to go on forever, six lines being asked for as the learner reached the end of the written ones, but each request writes an independent text with its own beginning and end (`story.py` says so in its system prompt), so what was appended was a second story rather than a continuation — invisible while only three lines were on screen, plain as a break now that the whole text stands there. Carrying the text written so far into the request and asking the model to continue it is the other way out, and it is not taken: a story that ends is a place to stop, and the learner asks for the next one.

    **The learner sets the size of a pass** (user, 2026-09-24), on the start card of the mode it belongs to and never while a pass is running: Ord asks how many words the pass holds (3, 5, 10 or 20) and how often each of them comes back (1, 2 or 3), Meningar how many lines the story has (3, 6 or 10). Each option is a row of a few values rather than a number to type, since the page asks for a tap and not for a decision about what a good number would be. The answers live in `frontend/src/lib/options.ts` (`takk.options` in `localStorage`, beside the progress store), so a learner who practises the same way every day picks it once. A repeated word comes back with the other words of the pass in between rather than straight after itself (`pass`, unit tested in `options.test.ts`): signing it twice in a row tests reading the clip, not remembering the sign.

    **A word can always be taken out of the vocabulary again** (user, 2026-09-24), in both places and by one rule: tapping a picked tile in Sök unpicks it whatever box it is in, and every row of the list under Tecken carries a × that drops the sign. Removing takes what was learned of the sign with it and is not confirmed: the sign can be picked again, and only its box is lost. Step 9's lock on a practised sign is gone with it — it left "Nollställ" as the only way to drop a word, which throws away the whole vocabulary to lose one word. `remove` in `progress.ts` is the one place it happens.

    **Tecken shows the signs, not a table of words** (user, 2026-09-24: the word list "is a bit boring"). The list became a board: a card per word with the lexicon's clip, the word, and a dot per box so how far it has climbed is read at a glance, grouped under the step of the schedule it sits at ("nya", "imorgon", "3 dagar", …) in the order a word climbs them. The board says what the bar chart above it says, with the signs in it. A word the schedule asks for now carries a "nu" chip and an accent border. The clips are still until the card is pointed at, since a page of vocabulary would otherwise be fifty moving pictures, and each is loaded with a `#t=0.1` fragment so the card shows a frame of the sign rather than a black box. A group with no words is left out; the zeroes are read off the chart.

    **The app never praises the learner** (user, 2026-09-24: "There's no need for positive feedback"). A recording the checks found nothing wrong with used to answer "Det ser bra ut.", which Sök wrote under the camera after every search by signing; `NOTES["ok"]` in `practice.py` is now the empty string, so nothing is written. What the app still says is the verdict of an attempt and whatever the learner has to fix — the framing, the reasons a recording could not be used — which is how `framing()` was already built: it returns nothing when the framing is right.

    The rule is about wording rather than about acknowledgement: the same day the user asked for **a mark on a word signed right in Ord**, since the card advanced the instant the word was accepted and the learner never saw that it had been. **A verdict in Ord is that mark and nothing else** (user, 2026-09-24): the word turns green with a tick or red with a cross for 700 ms, and neither "Rätt" nor "Fel — det såg inte ut som …" is written any more. The second said nothing the learner could act on — the sign a recording happened to look like is not why it was wrong, and the reference clip beside them is what tells them. The mark keeps its place in the heading whether or not it is shown, so the word does not move when it lands. `Verdict.svelte` is left with the cases where there is no verdict at all: a recording that could not be used, and the recorder's own message. Meningar already marks this way, colouring the words of the line it scored.

    **Nothing is pressed to record** (user, 2026-09-24). The "Spela in igen" button under the camera is gone: the recorder arms itself when a card appears, starts on the voice, ends on the silence after it, arms again when a wait runs long, and the pages arm it again after a verdict, so the button only ever restarted a recording that was already running — which the space bar does. The phases are not written out either — "Lyssnar på rummet…", "Säg meningen när du är redo." and "Hör dig — teckna medan du talar." are gone with the seconds counter, and what the recorder is doing is the red outline on the picture. The outline is on while the voice is being recorded and not before: waiting for the learner to speak captures nothing they would want back, and an outline that is on throughout says nothing. Sök outlines the picture the same way while its own recording runs. What is still written under the camera is the missing microphone and "Bedömer …". A take already being spoken can no longer be abandoned without a keyboard; it runs to silence and is scored, which in Ord is a miss and simply means signing the word again.

    **A level strip under the camera shows the microphone being heard** (user, 2026-09-25: "some kind of visualization showing that we're listening to the user"). The outline alone said nothing while the recorder waited for the voice, so a learner could not tell a waiting recorder from a dead one. `Level.svelte`, under the camera in Träna only, draws the last two seconds of `Tracker.level` as bars, one per 50 ms reading, while the recorder listens (calibrating, armed or speaking): dim while it waits and red, like the outline, once the voice is being recorded. While an attempt is judged the recorder is not listening, and the strip stands still on the take that was heard until the next arming rather than disappearing (user, 2026-09-25): it blinked out on every verdict, and bars moving then would claim a hearing nothing uses. The height is the square root of the level, so a quiet room still flickers. Its space is held when it is hidden, so the page does not move. It is a level history rather than an oscilloscope of the raw samples, which cover 21 ms and read as noise, and it draws no threshold line, which would be an internal number. — done, checked with `npm run check`. Not tried in a browser yet.

    **The hands start and end a search by signing** (user, 2026-09-24). Its button was the last recording in the app that both began and ended by hand; now raising the hands into the picture records and lowering them searches. `frontend/src/lib/hands.ts` is the same shape as `voice.ts` — a reading is one tracked frame, the hands are there when either hand's landmarks were tracked at all, three readings with them start the recording and fourteen without them (0.7 s) end it, so a hand the tracker loses mid-sign does not cut it short (unit tested in `hands.test.ts`). A recording is cut at `maxSeconds` for hands that are never lowered, the next sign cannot begin until the lookup is done, and the red outline on the picture is what says a recording is running.

    **Sök has no two ways any more** (user, 2026-09-24): the camera stands under the search field the whole time and the "Sök med tecken" switch is gone, since a sign starts a search by itself and there is nothing left to be in a mode of. The learner types a word or signs one, and both fill the same grid. The picture stands centred at the top of the page with the field under it, and the field's placeholder ("Teckna ordet, eller sök efter ord eller teman") is where both ways are said (user, 2026-09-24), so the page carries no line of instructions of its own. **"Lägg till alla" is gone as well** (user, 2026-09-24): a search answers with twenty signs of a theme and adding all of them was never what the learner meant, so a sign enters the vocabulary one tile at a time; what stands above the results now is only how many there are. **The replay of the sign they made is gone with it** (user, 2026-09-24): the picture stays live, and the results are what answers the sign. The `overlay` hook on the camera, which existed for that replay alone, is gone too. This narrows step 17's rule that the camera only opens where it is used: Sök is such a place at all times, and Tecken still opens none.

    **A result's clip is still until it is pointed at** (user, 2026-09-24, reporting that part of the page went black once results arrived). Thirty clips playing at once, beside the camera and the landmarker, is more video than a browser will decode: whichever elements it gives up on render black, and the camera can be one of them. The tiles now load with `preload="metadata"` and a `#t=0.1` fragment so each shows a frame of the sign, and play only while the pointer is on them — the same treatment the board under Tecken gets, for the same reason.

    Sök shows its results as a grid of clips, a whole tile being the control that picks the sign, since a word is recognised by its sign rather than by the row it sits in. Tecken leads with the three numbers, then the charts, then the words with the interval each has reached and when it comes back — the list is new, and it is what "an overview of the words being practised" asked for. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`, and with headless screenshots of the shell. Not practised through in a browser yet: the camera, the recording and the verdicts are unchanged code, but the new layout around them has not been used with a live camera.

18. **Deployment: the page on GitHub Pages, the API on Cloud Run** (user, 2026-09-24) — planned. The two halves are already separate deployments (see the decisions); this is what it takes to actually run them. The model the API serves comes from the bundle of step 12 of ROADMAP.md, which is what keeps the training data out of the image.

    **No GPU.** Measured on this machine with the thread count a Cloud Run instance would have, for a 3 s recording: 1.12 s on one vCPU and 0.66 s on two, of which the forced alignment is about 95% and the sign model 46–78 ms (see findings). Two vCPU, 4 GB and a concurrency of about 4 is the shape; a GPU instance would pay for hardware the app cannot use.

    **What the API needs before it can be an image at all.** CORS, which has never been configured and which every cross-origin call needs (the open question of step 5); binding `0.0.0.0:$PORT` instead of the `127.0.0.1:8002` default; and a dependency split, since `pyproject.toml` has one list and the image would otherwise carry mediapipe, OpenCV, matplotlib, scikit-learn and yt-dlp. The split needs `VideoInfo` moved out of `extraction.py`, which is the only reason `practice.py` imports a module that pulls in mediapipe. `ffmpeg` is a real runtime dependency — `speech.py` shells out to it to decode the recording's audio — and an apt package rather than a wheel.

    **The image** is `python:3.13-slim` + ffmpeg + the CPU torch wheels + `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish` (1.2 GB) baked into `HF_HOME` in a layer of its own, plus the ~40 MB serving bundle: about 2.5 GB, of which the speech model is nearly all. The bundle is **copied into the image rather than mounted** from the bucket with Cloud Storage FUSE (user, 2026-09-24, for the upload only): baking keeps a revision a full description of what it serves, so a rollback is a revision, and it removes a boot-time dependency on the bucket. FUSE becomes interesting if bundles grow past a few hundred MB or retraining gets frequent enough that rebuilding the image is the annoyance; the speech model stays in the image either way, since loading 1.2 GB over FUSE at every cold start is the one thing that would hurt. Startup after that is the 3.2 s the aligner takes plus the image pull, so `min-instances=1` is what keeps a learner from waiting for it.

    **The page on GitHub Pages** needs `paths.base`, because a project site is served under `/<repo>/` and the app has six hardcoded `href="/…"` plus `goto("/träna/ord")`; a custom domain or a `<user>.github.io` repository avoids all of it. `fallback` becomes `404.html`, which is how Pages serves a single-page app. `VITE_API_BASE` is set at build time to the Cloud Run URL.

    **The build runs in GitHub Actions**, not locally and not in Cloud Build: Pages needs Actions anyway, Workload Identity Federation removes the service-account key, and one place then shows both deploys. Cloud Build is the fallback if the hosted runner's free disk cannot hold the ~2.5 GB image. Two workflows: one on `frontend/**` that runs `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build` and deploys the site, and one on `src/**`, the Dockerfile and `deploy/bundle.txt` that runs `uv run pytest` (148 tests, 26 s, no datasets needed), fetches the bundle named by `deploy/bundle.txt` from the secret bucket, builds, pushes to Artifact Registry and deploys the Cloud Run revision. CI never trains and never touches a dataset.

    — the local image is done (2026-09-24, the first step the user asked for, before any cloud piece exists). `Dockerfile` builds it from the repo root around a bundle named by `--build-arg BUNDLE=`, and `.dockerignore` keeps the build context to the four things it copies, since `data/` and `outputs/` are tens of gigabytes. It runs: `/api/signs` answers with lexicon addresses, `/api/search` and `/api/form` answer, `/api/story` answers with no parts when no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is passed, and a cross-origin request gets `access-control-allow-origin: *`.

    **The image takes CPU torch from PyTorch's own index, not the lockfile's wheels** — the first build was 14.2 GB, because `uv sync --frozen` installs what the machine trains with, and the CUDA runtime is 12 GB of that. The image resolves the lockfile's versions with `uv export` and installs them with `--index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu`, dropping the `nvidia-*` and `triton` lines, so the versions stay pinned while the wheels carry no CUDA. That brought it to **4.8 GB**: 1.6 GB virtual environment (769 MB torch, 172 MB polars, 114 MB transformers, 109 MB scipy, 48 MB scikit-learn), 1.2 GB speech model, 39 MB bundle, the rest the Debian base with ffmpeg. Trimming further means dropping scikit-learn (transformers imports it) or pruning torch, and neither is worth doing before the pull time is a measured problem.

    — the page's half is done too (2026-09-24). The user chose the project site over a custom domain, so the build takes the prefix from `BASE_PATH` (`/<repo>`, which the workflow reads from the repository's own name) and every link goes through `base` from `$app/paths`; the two layouts strip it off `page.url.pathname` in the one place they derive the current path from, so the route names they compare against stay written as they are. The fallback is `404.html`, which is the page Pages serves for a path it holds no file for, and `static/.nojekyll` keeps the `_app` directory from being taken for Jekyll's. `.github/workflows/pages.yml` runs `npm ci`, `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build` and deploys the artifact; `VITE_API_BASE` is a repository variable, so the site can be deployed before the API has a URL and pointed at it afterwards. Pages has to be turned on once, with "GitHub Actions" as the source.

    **The image is built by Cloud Build from a connected repository, not by GitHub Actions** (user, 2026-09-24, asking whether Cloud Run's "connect to repo" would do). It does, and it removes the whole Workload Identity Federation, deployer service account and GitHub secret half of the plan: the trigger holds the substitutions and Cloud Build's own service account holds the credentials, so nothing about GCP is stored in GitHub. The one thing the wizard's plain build cannot do is fetch the serving bundle, which is not in the repository, so the build is a `cloudbuild.yaml` instead: it downloads the archive `deploy/bundle.txt` names from the private bucket, checks it against the digest beside it, builds, pushes to Artifact Registry and deploys the revision. The alternatives were committing the 39 MB bundle to git, which grows the history by that much per model, and downloading it at container start, which gives up a revision describing what it serves. GitHub Actions keeps the page and the Python tests and touches no cloud.

    **Publishing a model is an upload and a commit** (see step 12 of ROADMAP.md): `scripts/build_serving.py --publish` tars the bundle, names it by its own sha256, uploads it to `$TAKK_BUNDLE_URI` with `gcloud storage cp` and rewrites `deploy/bundle.txt`. Committing that one line is what deploys it, so the history says which model each revision served and a rollback is an older pin. The bucket is in the environment and in a trigger substitution, never in the repository (user, 2026-09-24).

    **It runs on the free tier** (user, 2026-09-24), which decides three things. `--min-instances` is 0, since the free tier covers request-based billing only, so the first request after an idle period waits for the image pull and the 3.2 s the speech model takes to load. The build uses Cloud Build's default machine type, the only one the free build minutes cover. And the API now gzips: `/api/signs` is 2.32 MB of JSON that every page load fetches, 271 kB compressed, so a gigabyte of egress went from about 430 page loads to about 3,700 (`GZipMiddleware`, measured on the served glossary). It is not. The model thinks before it writes and the thinking counts against `max_tokens`, so a turn that runs out of budget comes back with the thinking alone and no text block at all, and `parsed_output` is then None. It is intermittent by nature — a longer vocabulary means more thinking — which is why the same endpoint answered a story the day before and failed twice in a row here, at 24 s and 27 s against the 15 s of the request that worked. `max_tokens` went from 2,000 to 16,000, which is the SDK's recommended floor for a non-streaming request, and a missing `parsed_output` is now a try that failed like any other: the loop asks again and the endpoint answers with no parts, which is the answer the page already knows how to show.

19. **Meningar in natural Swedish** (user, 2026-09-25). TAKK signs the key words of ordinary spoken Swedish, so a story that may not inflect them is writing a language nobody speaks — and it was failing besides: measured over ten word lists, Sonnet 5 wrote a story that passed the contract on the first ask 2 times in 10, four fifths of the failures being a definite or inflected form ("bilen", "Brödet … äter") that left its part signing nothing. Three constraints went, and one of them was the whole reason the answer used to be checkable:

    **The writer inflects freely and reports what it wrote.** Each sign of a part is now `said`, the form exactly as it stands in the text, and `word`, the offered word that form signs. `signed_words` checks what can still be checked — the form really is in the part, the forms are in the order they are spoken, and the word was one of the offered — and trusts the pairing itself, because no cheap rule relates a Swedish surface form to its base ("åt" shares nothing with "äta"). So a compound claimed as its part ("blåbär" reported as *blå*) passes the validator; the system prompt forbids it and nothing enforces it. The form is what is spoken, so it is what `speech.py` times in the audio and what the page marks; the word is what names the sign and moves the box. `/api/attempt` needed no change at all — it has always taken `spoken` per sign.

    **The number of parts is what the story aims at, not a contract.** A story one part short is a shorter pass rather than a story to throw away, and the page has no use for an exact count.

    **A word said twice in a part is signed twice.** Forced alignment is positional — `word_targets` lays the words out as one target sequence with a wildcard between them — so two occurrences are two targets, two spans and two verdicts, and the server needed nothing for this either. The page did: verdicts are now read back by position instead of by the sign they scored, which was already wrong wherever two of the learner's words are one sign form, and `pieces` walks the reported forms in order rather than searching the whole text for each, so each occurrence carries the index of the sign it is. A word signed twice in one line is still **one answer** — correct only if every occurrence was — so the box still moves once per pass.

    **The story is asked for by length, not by a number of lines** (user, 2026-09-25: "let's not have the granular number of parts control in the frontend"). The start card offered kort (3 parts) and lång (10); later the same day the choice was removed and a story is always lång, 10 parts (user, 2026-09-25: "let's remove the kort/lång option for the story and always go for lång"). A number of lines is an internal unit the learner has no way to reason about, and with the count now loose it promised a precision the system does not keep. "Antal ord" and "Repetitioner" on Ord are untouched for now. — done, checked with `uv run pytest`, `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. Not practised through in a browser yet, which is the next thing: the contract, the marking and the box all changed on a path that only a real recording exercises.

## Decisions

- **The camera says how the signer sits, not how fast it runs** (user, 2026-09-21). The page carries the framing feedback of step 11 and nothing about frames per second.

- **The blueprint is the layout, and the pages stay very minimal** (user, 2026-09-21). Each screen holds what its box in the map holds, in that order, and nothing beside it, and it shows the thing rather than explaining it: no page tells the learner how the mode they are in works. No icons, no cards or shadows, no colour beyond what marks a state, no UI framework or component library, and no headings or helper text the map has no room for. Charts are the exception (user, 2026-09-21): a charting library is fine where it makes the graphs much better. Where the map labels something in Swedish ("Sök efter ord eller teman", "Lägg till alla", "Nollställ"), that wording is the label.

    **Step 17 replaced the layout half of this** (user, 2026-09-23): the map no longer dictates the arrangement, and panels, a rail and a two-column practice screen are now what the app is made of. What still holds is the rest: the pages explain nothing about how a mode works, colour marks a state and little else, there is no UI framework or component library, no icons, and the map's Swedish wording is still the wording.

- **The learner chooses the vocabulary by searching the lexicon** (user, 2026-09-21). Ready-made lists — the starter packs and the lexicon's categories as a picker — are dropped in favour of search, by word or by theme or by signing. The categories remain underneath as what a theme search matches, which is what they were always good for: they are subject areas rather than a learning order (see findings), so they made a poor picker and make a reasonable search index.

- **The app is in Swedish, and keeps the learner's state in the browser until something needs more** (user, 2026-09-20). No accounts and no database for as long as this holds. The LLM steps are also the point of the exercise and not only a means, as the user wants the app to be an occasion to use LLM APIs.

- **The app is two modes and nothing else** (user, 2026-09-21): Ord, where a word is signed from its clip, and Meningar, where the words already learned are recalled from memory in a text. A word is taught with its clip the first time and whenever it has been missed since (step 16); a card says only whether the sign was right; nothing is pressed between cards.

- **Everything is spoken** (user, 2026-09-21). TAKK is signed while speaking, so every attempt is said aloud however few of its words are signed, and the microphone is required throughout — including a word being taught for the first time. The rule is uniform because a learner should not have to remember which kind of turn they are in, which is worth more than the two arguments for exempting the teaching turn (a clip is copied rather than recalled, and whole-recording scoring is more forgiving than alignment). The one exception is Sök med tecken, which is a lookup and not an attempt.

- **A pass always demotes, but promotes only a sign that was due** (user, 2026-09-21). A box is a claim about an interval — the third means "still remembered after seven days" — and an answer on the second day has not tested that, so promoting early would push the next repetition out to an interval that was never met, and a word could climb out of the boxes by being drilled in one sitting; it is why Anki does not reschedule "review ahead" by default. A miss is evidence whatever the day, since forgetting a sign that was not due means its interval was already too long. The asymmetry means an extra pass can only repair the boxes, never inflate them.

- **The model picks which of a turn's words to use, and inflects them as the sentence needs** (user, 2026-09-21, and 2026-09-25 for the inflection). The words are offered with only the first required, since deciding the combination in the app produced sentences that listed words instead of saying something (see findings). Inflection was forbidden at first, so that the whole answer could be read off the text and checked (every word a whole token, once) rather than reported; the escape written down here then — "if the texts turn out stilted, the model returning `{word, spoken}` pairs is the change to make" — is exactly what step 19 did, and for that reason. The words are still timed in the audio by the form that was spoken, which is now the inflected one. A text that breaks the contract is asked for again once, with what was wrong.

- **A learner's choices never go in the URL** (user, 2026-09-21): what is being practised is chosen in the page, before a pass starts.

- **The frontend is a SvelteKit app, built and hosted apart from the API** (user, 2026-09-20: a real learning app is coming, with lesson flow, progress and phone use, and a Node build step is acceptable). `frontend/` holds it (SvelteKit 2, Svelte 5, TypeScript, Vite, `adapter-static`, prerendered with `ssr = false`), outside the Python package so that `node_modules` and the build never reach the wheel. The API does not serve it: Vite's dev and preview servers proxy `/api` to `127.0.0.1:8002`, so only one port is forwarded from this machine, and a deployed build reaches the API through `VITE_API_BASE`. The user rejected serving the built files from FastAPI as coupling that hurts deployment and availability.

- **The large unchanging files the browser downloads ("media") are meant for a static host, not the API process**: `holistic_landmarker.task` (13.7 MB, `/api/model`) and the lexicon clips (17 GB in `data/raw/sts-lexikon/movies`). They are read off disk, while `POST /api/attempt` is what needs torch, the checkpoint and the ~45 s startup. The design rule is that the API hands the frontend URLs for clips and the model rather than paths it assembles itself, so they can be repointed at a CDN server-side. The goal is that searching the lexicon and watching clips keep working while the scoring API is asleep — which step 9 makes more important, since search is now how vocabulary grows. Both are off the API now: the clips (below) and the landmarker.

- **The landmarker model is loaded from MediaPipe's own address** (user, 2026-09-24). `tracking.ts` points `modelAssetPath` at `https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/.../float16/latest/holistic_landmarker.task`, the URL `extraction.py` downloads from, as the wasm beside it already comes from jsdelivr; `/api/model` is gone and the API no longer keeps a copy of the 13.7 MB. Google answers a cross-origin GET with `access-control-allow-origin: *`, so the browser may fetch it. The alternative was a copy in `frontend/static/`, which would pin the version the browser extracts with; the user chose the upstream address, so the risk is that `latest` moves under the app and browser landmarks drift from `extraction.py` — the comparison of browser against server landmarks in ROADMAP.md is what would catch it. It is also cached for an hour rather than a month, so a returning learner may fetch it again.

- **The clips are played from Svenskt teckenspråkslexikon itself** (user, 2026-09-24). `/api/signs` sends each sign's clips as `https://teckensprakslexikon.su.se/movies/...` (`sts_lexikon.video_urls`, built from the `video` path the crawl kept), and `/api/reference/<clip id>` is gone with the copy it served. The lexicon sends them `public, max-age=2592000, immutable` and does not check the referer, so the browser caches them and the 17 GB never passes through this process. Two consequences: watching a clip now needs the internet rather than only this machine, and the local copy under `data/raw/sts-lexikon/movies` is kept for landmark extraction alone. A glossary other than the lexicon (own recordings) would have no addresses and show no clips.

- **The app applies the threshold, it does not choose it.** What an attempt has to reach is decided from the model's numbers, in the decisions of ROADMAP.md (a provisional 0.38, `--threshold`).

- **Judging a sentence sign by sign while it is signed was built and then removed** (user, 2026-09-20). It worked: a verdict appeared about one sign behind the signer, from a rest or from the next key word being spoken. The commits are in the history (reverted in `a0d3e0a` and the three before it) if it is ever wanted again.

## Findings

- **Which model writes the story, and how hard it thinks** (2026-09-25, `scripts/compare_story_models.py`, ten word lists per configuration). First-ask success against the old strict contract: Opus 5 at low effort 7 in 10, at medium 8 in 10; Sonnet 5 two in ten at either. Median ask 5.4 s (Opus low), 8.4 s (Opus medium), about 4 s (Sonnet). Sonnet's speed does not survive the retry loop: including one retry it is about 7.2 s to Opus low's 7.0 s, and only 36% of stories ever come back against 91%. Sonnet also dropped an offered word from the story entirely in 5 of 20 runs, which no validator catches — Opus in none. `claude-opus-5` at `effort: "low"` was the choice (user, 2026-09-25), until the user switched the writer back to `claude-sonnet-5`, still at low effort, later the same day, after step 19 loosened the contract its failures came from; medium's one extra success is within the noise of ten trials and costs 3 s an ask. Every ask ended `end_turn`, so the 500 below was the token ceiling alone and nothing else. The numbers were measured before step 19 loosened the contract, so they understate both models.

- **An attempt costs about a second of CPU, and nearly all of it is the speech model** (2026-09-24, measured for the deployment of step 18). A 3 s recording scored end to end on this machine, with torch limited to the threads a small cloud instance would have: one thread 1.04 s alignment + 0.002 s preparation + 0.078 s embedding = 1.12 s; two threads 0.61 + 0.002 + 0.046 = 0.66 s. Alignment scales with the length of the audio, so a ten-second sentence is roughly 3.5 s on one thread and 2 s on two. The sign model itself is a rounding error, which is why the app needs no GPU and why making it faster means making the alignment faster, not the encoder. The process holds 2.1 GB resident, 641 MB of which is the prepared frames that the serving bundle of step 12 of ROADMAP.md removes.

- **The word a learner practises is not the name of the sign that scores it**, and every place that forgets it breaks (2026-09-20 and 2026-09-21). Entries of one sign form are one class labelled by its lowest entry, so "äta" is scored as `sts:livsmedel-01265`, "blå" as `sts:öga-02636` and "grön" as `sts:land-00416`. The flash cards, the progress page and the story each had to be fixed for it separately: a story once had the model write about "öga" while the learner was practising "blå", the sentence passed validation, nothing was marked, the alignment listened for the wrong word and the verdict was "Inga händer syntes". The word, the lexicon entry and the sign are kept apart everywhere, and `POST /api/attempt` takes the learner's own `spoken` word per sign.

- **JavaScript's `\b` is ASCII-only, so Swedish words were left unmarked** (user, 2026-09-21, found on their first story). "å" is not a word character, so `\bblå\b` matches nothing at all and the page printed the part without marking blå while the server counted, scored and recorded it. Any word ending or beginning in å, ä or ö was affected. The split lives in `frontend/src/lib/text.ts` with the boundary written out as `[\p{L}\p{N}_]` lookarounds, and is unit tested on exactly these words.

- **Forced alignment never fails, so a sentence signed in silence was scored as if it had been spoken** (user, 2026-09-21). `forced_align` is a Viterbi path: it returns an alignment for any audio, and the wildcard token makes one cheap to find, so silence gives each key word a narrow span somewhere and the split cuts between them. The user signed two signs without speaking and got two verdicts, both accepted. The alignment's own score tells the cases apart: against silence or room noise the words score -5.4 to -3.9 (measured with "hej" and "mamma"), while a word actually spoken scores near 0. `MIN_WORD_SCORE` is -3.0, between the two. The threshold is provisional in the same way as the acceptance threshold of ROADMAP.md — mumbling and a noisy room have not been measured — so the server prints the scores of every refused sentence for as long as that holds.

- **Splitting at the rests could not tell a skipped sign from a failed one**, which is why the speech split is now the only one (user, 2026-09-21). The rest split (`split_signs`, runs of frames with a raised hand at least 0.4 s apart) answered a skipped sign by voiding the whole sentence, losing the verdicts on the signs that were right, while the speech split cuts by the word's place in the audio and makes a skipped sign an ordinary miss. Keeping both would have meant an "unless the rest split ran" clause in every rule of the repetition schedule, since a miss and a void have opposite effects on a box. It measured well on the 51 Swedish browser recordings — every one of the 1,275 pairs joined end to end split into exactly two, with the hands lost for at most 0.23 s inside a sign against at least 0.43 s between two — so it was accuracy that was never the problem.

- **A word could reach the third box in a single pass** (user, 2026-09-21). A sign was put back in the queue whenever it was in the first box after an answer, which was meant to catch a missed sign but also caught a *new* word answered correctly, since a first accept moves a word from no box to box 1; and the record button stayed enabled after a verdict, so recording the same card again counted as a second answer. The rule that replaced it: the box moves once per pass, and a second recording of the same card is a retry that shows a verdict and changes nothing.

- **Imposing the combination made the sentences worse than offering it** (user, 2026-09-21). Asked for a sentence containing both "röd" and "blå", the model wrote "Nallen har en röd napp och ett öga." — two objects invented to carry the two colours, which is a list with grammar rather than a sentence about anything. The words are now offered with only the head required, so the same pair can produce "Har du en röd tröja?" and blå waits for a turn it fits. Reading the set off the text makes the validation smaller rather than larger: the head must be there, no word twice, at most three in all.

- **What a theme should offer first is `lexicon_hits`, and two traps sit in the lexicon's words** (2026-09-20). Of the three counts the entry pages publish, `lexicon_hits` is on every entry and its head is what TAKK teaches (Djur: hund, katt, djur; Kläder: kläder, sko, tröja; Sport: träna, fotboll, ishockey). `corpus_hits` exists for only 6,003 of 21,692 entries, and `survey_hits` is close to an anti-signal, since the surveys ask about rare signs and put avocado and hamster first. The first trap is the word-versus-sign-name one above: ordered naively, "Djur" began at "hane" and "batteri". The second is Österberg's 1916 dictionary, 417 entries of historical sign forms in a category of their own, which must never be taught — "hjälp" resolved into one of them until a heading was made to beat another entry's `also` wording. The lexicon's 14,343 category memberships come out as 9,887 words.

- **The lexicon's categories are subject areas, not a learning order** (2026-09-20). By size they are Sport 2,238 signs, Geografi 1,524, Sjukvård 1,136, Egennamn 757, and only then Barn 320, Kläder 131, Känslor 92, Familj 74, Hälsningsfras 14, so a beginner cannot start from them — which is what the starter packs existed for, and why search replaced both. What a beginner needs is covered, though: 38 of 39 words of a typical TAKK starter vocabulary resolve to a lexicon sign (only "godnatt" is missing). The categories are read off the entry pages by `parse_entry`, which takes the whole page in one pass (user, 2026-09-20: download everything about the dataset once, rather than adding a second file per field), so it also reads the entry's other wording, its English translation and its hit counts.

- **Timing the spoken words costs one model and half a second** (`takk/speech.py`). Forced alignment needs the words, not a transcript, which is exactly what a TAKK sentence already is. KBLab's `easyaligner` (https://github.com/kb-labb/easyaligner, MIT) is the same recipe packaged but was not adopted: it pins `torch>=2.7,<2.9` against this project's 2.14 and is built for batch alignment over directories, with `pyannote-audio` and `silero-vad` that its own documentation says alignment does not use. Only `torchaudio.functional.forced_align` is used here, plus the wildcard applied between the words rather than only at the ends. A wildcard column of zeros is the highest possible log probability, so each key word gets close to the fewest frames it can and comes out as a narrow span at its clearest frames, which is enough to cut between them. `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish` is 1.2 GB, loads in 2.8 s and aligns a 4 s recording in 0.4 s on the CPU.

- **The app end to end** (headless Chrome with the `sju` recording as a fake webcam, `--use-file-for-fake-video-capture`, a 2.9 s recording each): signing sju for sju is accepted with score 0.80; the same attempt for tupp scores 0.05 and for klassifikator 0.27, both rejected. Extraction without a seek per frame takes about 7 s for 2.9 s of video, so the answer comes about 7 s after the recording stops. Live tracking in that browser reaches only 2–6 fps (WebGL on SwiftShader), so it shows that the page works, not how fast it is on a real GPU; even at 2 fps an attempt of sju was accepted (0.81) and the same video for tupp rejected (0.06).
