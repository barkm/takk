# Roadmap — the TAKK practice app

Status of the app and the plan ahead, as ROADMAP.md holds them for the isolated sign verification work the app is built on (`src/isolated_sign_validation/`). Update this file when a step is finished, a decision is made, or the plan changes. Read the roadmap of the package you touch, both when a change crosses. See README.md for how to run the app.

The app (user, 2026-09-19): practising TAKK (tecken som alternativ och kompletterande kommunikation), from single signs to sentences. TAKK signs the key words of spoken Swedish with STS lexicon signs, in Swedish word order and closer to isolation than fluent STS, so a sentence is a known sequence of lexicon signs to find and score in order. What remains is splitting the recording into signs, possibly slightly blended forms, and errors adding up over a sentence's signs.

## Steps

1. **Single signs** (`takk/practice.py`, `takk/main.py`, `takk/web/practice.html`, see README): search the lexicon, watch a sign's clip, sign it to the webcam. The browser tracks the landmarks live (user decision, 2026-09-19: the GPU delegate, fully live): VIDEO mode on every camera frame it keeps up with, skipping the frames that arrive while it is busy, drawn over the camera image, with the rate it reaches shown (a warning below 20 fps); a recording keeps each result with its capture time and is resampled to 30 fps (the latest tracked frame at each step, as the collection app's transcode picks frames). The server checks, prepares (mirrored by the attempt's dominant hand, or the user's stated handedness for two-handed attempts), embeds with `iv14_h384_e20` and accepts when the mean cosine similarity to the sign's lexicon clips reaches the threshold (see decisions); it also returns the closest sign of the whole lexicon. The lexicon's embeddings are cached next to the run. Nothing is stored. — done, checked end to end in headless Chrome (see findings). Open: the real frame rate on the user's GPU, and whether results hold up when frames are skipped.

2. **Sentences of several signs**: picking more words extends the sentence, and the recording is split at the rests between the signs (`split_signs`: runs of frames with a raised hand at least `MIN_REST` = 0.4 s apart, halved into the rests around them) and scored sign by sign, with a verdict for each. A recording of one sign is never split, and a sentence whose number of signs found differs from the expected one is not scored at all. — done (see findings)

3. **Splitting a spoken sentence by its words** instead of by the rests (user decision, 2026-09-19: TAKK is spoken while signed, so take advantage of it). The browser records the microphone alongside the landmarks, and `takk/speech.py` times the sentence's key words in the audio by forced alignment (`torchaudio.functional.forced_align` over `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish`), cutting halfway from one word's end to the next word's start. The words are known, so nothing is recognized; a wildcard token between and around them absorbs the rest of the spoken sentence, so only the key words have to be said as written and the signer speaks naturally. Without a microphone or without `--no-speech`'s model the rests still split the recording, and the page says which of the two found the signs. — done: the user confirmed it works in their own browser, speaking Swedish sentences to it (2026-09-19); the split's own tests use synthetic emissions (see findings)

4. **The page rewritten as a SvelteKit app** (`frontend/`, see the decisions): the same single screen, ported component by component, with the camera, the landmarker loop, the common landmark layout and the API calls in typed modules (`src/lib/tracking.ts`, `landmarks.ts`, `api.ts`) that the components only render. `src/takk/web/practice.html` and the server's `GET /` are gone; `takk` is the API alone. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`, and the user confirmed the rewritten page works in their own browser (2026-09-20). Open: CORS on the API (`allow_origin`), needed the first time the page is served from another origin than the API.

Steps 5 and on are the learning experience, planned with the user on 2026-09-20 and not started. They are ordered so that each one is useful on its own and the state the app keeps grows only when something needs it. Throughout, the app's numbers rest on the self-recorded Swedish clips of step 10 of ROADMAP.md, so they grow with that set: more signs and signers before any result from the app means much.

5. **The app in Swedish**: every string the learner reads, in `frontend/src/lib/*.svelte`. Hardcoded Swedish, no translation framework, because the app is for practising TAKK and has no other audience. — planned

6. **Word sets**: a handful of categories a beginner actually starts from (mat, kläder, familj, känslor, vardagsrutiner, ...), each a list of lexicon ids, as one data file in the repository. TAKK practice needs on the order of a hundred signs, not the lexicon's 21,680, and a set is what onboarding, recommendation and a session are all picked from, so nothing above this step can be built before it. — planned. Open: whether the sets are handpicked or labelled once by an LLM over the lexicon's Swedish words (`scripts/`, output committed), since the lexicon crawl has no categories of its own.

7. **Progress and spaced repetition in the browser**: per sign a Leitner box and a next-due date in `localStorage`, moved up by an accepted attempt and back to the first box by a rejected one. No accounts, no database and no user state on the server for as long as this holds; export and import of the file covers a second device. — planned

8. **A practice session**: "dagens pass", a few due signs from step 7 and a couple of new ones from a chosen set of step 6. A pure function over the two steps below it, and the point at which the app stops being a single screen and becomes something to come back to. — planned

9. **Sentences written by an LLM** (the first use of an LLM API in the project): `POST /api/sentence` turns a set of due words into a Swedish sentence whose key words are all signs in the lexicon. The answer is validated against the lexicon and asked for again when a key word has no sign, which is what makes this a small, checkable first LLM surface. — planned. Open: whether the sentences are generated per request or once, offline into a data file, which decides whether a deployed app needs an API key at all.

10. **Free signing scored against what was said**: instead of a given sentence, the learner speaks and signs freely, and the app reports how many of the key words were signed. The speech model already loaded by `takk/speech.py` is a CTC model, so decoding its emissions gives the transcript; the content words of that transcript that exist in the lexicon become the expected signs, and the existing forced alignment and `split_signs` scoring run unchanged. — planned

11. **Conversation mode** (user, 2026-09-20): a chat with an LLM in which the learner answers by speaking and signing, and gets their signing corrected turn by turn. Two ways to run a turn, and the mode should support both: *guided*, where the LLM writes the sentence to speak and sign (step 9) and the app scores it sign by sign as today, and *free*, where the LLM asks something and the learner answers in their own words, scored by step 10. Guided is the easier and the stricter of the two; free is the one that resembles using TAKK. The LLM sees which signs were accepted and rejected, so it can bring a missed sign back in its next turn, which is spaced repetition inside a conversation. — planned

12. **Onboarding as a chat**: an LLM asks about previous experience, already known signs, the reason for learning and the everyday context (barn, vård, förskola, ...) and turns the answers into chosen sets of step 6 and a starting state for step 7. It can only choose from sets that exist, so it follows step 6, not precedes it. — planned

13. **State on the server**: accounts and a database, when the browser's own storage of step 7 actually gets in the way (a second device, or more than one learner). Not before: a single table reached through a login is the kind of thing that is cheap to add late and expensive to carry early. — planned

## Decisions

- **The app is in Swedish, and keeps the learner's state in the browser until something needs more** (user, 2026-09-20). The learning experience is planned as steps 5 to 13 above: word sets and a practice session first, with no LLM and no server state, then the LLM steps (sentences, free signing, conversation, onboarding), and accounts and a database only when the browser's storage gets in the way. The LLM is also the point of the exercise and not only a means, as the user wants the app to be an occasion to use LLM APIs.

- **The frontend is a SvelteKit app, built and hosted apart from the API** (user decision, 2026-09-20: a real learning app is coming, with lesson flow, progress and phone use, and a Node build step is acceptable). `frontend/` holds it (SvelteKit 2, Svelte 5, TypeScript, Vite, `adapter-static`, prerendered with `ssr = false`), outside the Python package so that `node_modules` and the build never reach the wheel. The API does not serve it: Vite's dev and preview servers proxy `/api` to `127.0.0.1:8002`, so only one port is forwarded from this machine, and a deployed build reaches the API through `VITE_API_BASE`. The user rejected serving the built files from FastAPI as coupling that hurts deployment and availability.

- **The large unchanging files the browser downloads ("media") are meant for a static host, not the API process**: `holistic_landmarker.task` (13.7 MB, `/api/model`) and the lexicon clips (17 GB in `data/raw/sts-lexikon/movies`, `/api/reference/<clip id>`). They are read off disk, while `POST /api/attempt` is what needs torch, the checkpoint and the ~45 s startup that embeds the lexicon. Nothing moves yet; the design rule is that the API hands the frontend URLs for clips and the model rather than paths it assembles itself, so they can be repointed at a CDN server-side. The goal is that searching the lexicon and watching clips keep working while the scoring API is asleep.

- **The app applies the threshold, it does not choose it.** What an attempt has to reach is decided from the model's numbers, in the decisions of ROADMAP.md (a provisional 0.38, `--threshold`).

- **Judging a sentence sign by sign while it is signed was built and then removed** (user, 2026-09-20). It worked: a verdict appeared about one sign behind the signer, from a rest or from the next key word being spoken. The commits are in the history (reverted in `a0d3e0a` and the three before it) if it is ever wanted again.

## Findings

- Splitting a recording of several signs at the rests (`split_signs`, `MIN_REST` 0.4 s) on the 51 Swedish browser recordings: every recording is one sign, and every one of the 1,275 pairs joined end to end splits into exactly two. The margin is thin but real: inside a sign the hands are lost for at most 0.23 s, while the rest between two joined recordings (one's tail plus the next's lead) is at least 0.43 s, and deliberately lowering the hands between signs takes longer. The user confirmed the sentence mode works in their own browser (2026-09-19).

- Timing a TAKK sentence's spoken words instead of splitting at the rests (`takk/speech.py`): forced alignment needs the words, not a transcript, which is exactly what a sentence of lexicon signs already is, so no speech recognition is involved. KBLab's `easyaligner` (https://github.com/kb-labb/easyaligner, MIT) is the same recipe packaged, and the wildcard token is taken from it, but it was not adopted: it pins `torch>=2.7,<2.9` against this project's 2.14, and it is built for batch alignment of long recordings over directories of files, with `pyannote-audio` and `silero-vad` that its own documentation says alignment does not use. Its core is a thin wrapper over `torchaudio.functional.forced_align`, so only that is used here, plus the wildcard applied between the words rather than only at the ends of the transcript. A wildcard column of zeros is the highest possible log probability, so the alignment gives each key word close to the fewest frames it can and the wildcards everything else; the words therefore come out as narrow spans at their clearest frames, which is enough to cut between them. `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish` is 1.2 GB, loads in 2.8 s and aligns a 4 s recording in 0.4 s on the CPU. The user confirmed on real speech in their own browser that the words split the sentence correctly (2026-09-19).

- The practice app end to end (headless Chrome with the `sju` recording as a fake webcam, `--use-file-for-fake-video-capture`, a 2.9 s recording each): signing sju for sju is accepted with score 0.80; the same attempt for tupp scores 0.05 and for klassifikator 0.27, both rejected. Extraction without a seek per frame is faster: about 7 s for 2.9 s of video (about 13 fps), so the answer comes about 7 s after the recording stops. Embedding the 21,680 lexicon clips at startup takes about 45 s on the CPU.

- Live tracking in headless Chrome (a fake webcam playing the sju recording, WebGL on SwiftShader) reaches only 2–6 fps, so it shows that the page works, not how fast it is on a real GPU. Even at 2 fps an attempt of sju was accepted (score 0.81, closest sign pistolskytte at 0.81) and the same video for tupp rejected (0.06, closest sjunde at 0.83); the recording's phase in the looping fake video was not controlled.
