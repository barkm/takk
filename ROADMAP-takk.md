# Roadmap — the TAKK practice app

Status of the app and the plan ahead, as ROADMAP.md holds them for the isolated sign verification work the app is built on (`src/isolated_sign_validation/`). Update this file when a step is finished, a decision is made, or the plan changes. Read the roadmap of the package you touch, both when a change crosses. See README.md for how to run the app.

The app (user, 2026-09-19): practising TAKK (tecken som alternativ och kompletterande kommunikation), from single signs to sentences. TAKK signs the key words of spoken Swedish with STS lexicon signs, in Swedish word order and closer to isolation than fluent STS, so a sentence is a known sequence of lexicon signs to find and score in order. What remains is splitting the recording into signs, possibly slightly blended forms, and errors adding up over a sentence's signs.

## Steps

1. **Single signs** (`takk/practice.py`, `takk/main.py`, `takk/web/practice.html`, see README): search the lexicon, watch a sign's clip, sign it to the webcam. The browser tracks the landmarks live (user decision, 2026-09-19: the GPU delegate, fully live): VIDEO mode on every camera frame it keeps up with, skipping the frames that arrive while it is busy, drawn over the camera image, with the rate it reaches shown (a warning below 20 fps); a recording keeps each result with its capture time and is resampled to 30 fps (the latest tracked frame at each step, as the collection app's transcode picks frames). The server checks, prepares (mirrored by the attempt's dominant hand, or the user's stated handedness for two-handed attempts), embeds with `iv14_h384_e20` and accepts when the mean cosine similarity to the sign's lexicon clips reaches the threshold (see decisions); it also returns the closest sign of the whole lexicon. The lexicon's embeddings are cached next to the run. Nothing is stored. — done, checked end to end in headless Chrome (see findings). Open: the real frame rate on the user's GPU, and whether results hold up when frames are skipped.

2. **Sentences of several signs**: picking more words extends the sentence, and the recording is split into its signs and scored sign by sign, with a verdict for each. A recording of one sign is never split, and a sentence whose number of signs found differs from the expected one is not scored at all. — done (see findings). The split was at the rests between the signs to begin with (`split_signs`, runs of frames with a raised hand at least `MIN_REST` = 0.4 s apart); step 3 replaced it with the spoken words and then removed it.

3. **Splitting a spoken sentence by its words** instead of by the rests (user decision, 2026-09-19: TAKK is spoken while signed, so take advantage of it). The browser records the microphone alongside the landmarks, and `takk/speech.py` times the sentence's key words in the audio by forced alignment (`torchaudio.functional.forced_align` over `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish`), cutting halfway from one word's end to the next word's start. The words are known, so nothing is recognized; a wildcard token between and around them absorbs the rest of the spoken sentence, so only the key words have to be said as written and the signer speaks naturally. — done: the user confirmed it works in their own browser, speaking Swedish sentences to it (2026-09-19); the split's own tests use synthetic emissions (see findings).

   The speech split is now the only one (user, 2026-09-21), and `split_signs`, `MIN_REST` and `--no-speech` are gone. Two reasons. The rest split cannot tell a sign the signer skipped from a sign it failed to find, so it answered a skipped sign by voiding the whole sentence, losing the verdicts on the signs that were right; the speech split cuts by the word's place in the audio, so a skipped sign is an ordinary miss with its own verdict. And keeping both meant every rule of the practice session (step 9 and on) needed an "unless the rest split ran" clause, since a miss and a void have opposite effects on a Leitner box. A sentence now needs the microphone and says so before recording; a single sign is the whole recording (`split = "whole"`) and still works without it. The speech model is no longer optional, which costs 2.8 s of startup against the ~45 s the lexicon's embeddings take anyway. The removal also had to close a hole the rest split had been hiding: the alignment never fails, so a sentence signed in silence used to be cut at arbitrary places and scored (see the findings). Each word now carries its alignment score and a sentence whose words were not heard is refused. The user confirmed all three cases in their own browser (2026-09-21): a spoken sentence scores sign by sign, the same sentence signed in silence is refused, and a word said but not signed is a miss with a verdict of its own rather than voiding the sentence.

   **The recording begins and ends with the voice** (user, 2026-09-21), so nothing is pressed: `frontend/src/lib/voice.ts` reads the loudness of the newest 21 ms from an `AnalyserNode` on the microphone that is already open, fifty times a second, and `Recorder.svelte` arms a recording when the card appears, starts the attempt after three readings above the threshold and ends it after twenty-four under it. The quiet room is measured for 0.7 s first, since absolute levels mean nothing across microphones, and the threshold is three times that floor; a reading has to fall to six tenths of the threshold to count as quiet, so a soft last syllable does not cut the attempt short. Waiting armed for ten seconds throws the recording away and starts a new one, so a long wait is never sent. Every number is a knob. The user practised with it in their own browser on 2026-09-21 and it works, so the starting values are not far off, but none has been measured.

   The reading is deliberately crude, and that is the design: a window opened by a door has no spoken words in it, so alignment scores them under `MIN_WORD_SCORE` and the server refuses the attempt. A false start costs one window and never a wrong verdict, which is what pays for having no voice detector in the browser.

   Recording runs from when the card appears rather than from the first word, because the hands rise before the voice. What that leaves at the ends of the recording is dropped by the split rather than by the browser: `split_speech` used to run from the first frame to the last, so whatever was recorded before the signer began and after they finished was scored as part of the first and the last sign. Its outer edges now sit `PRE_ROLL` (0.5 s) before the first word and `POST_ROLL` (0.4 s) after the last. Forced alignment times the words exactly, so it is the better boundary in any case, and the fix stands on its own: it improved the button flow too.


4. **The page rewritten as a SvelteKit app** (`frontend/`, see the decisions): the same single screen, ported component by component, with the camera, the landmarker loop, the common landmark layout and the API calls in typed modules (`src/lib/tracking.ts`, `landmarks.ts`, `api.ts`) that the components only render. `src/takk/web/practice.html` and the server's `GET /` are gone; `takk` is the API alone. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`, and the user confirmed the rewritten page works in their own browser (2026-09-20). Open: CORS on the API (`allow_origin`), needed the first time the page is served from another origin than the API.

Steps 5 and on are the learning experience, planned with the user on 2026-09-20 and not started. They are ordered so that each one is useful on its own and the state the app keeps grows only when something needs it. Throughout, the app's numbers rest on the self-recorded Swedish clips of step 10 of ROADMAP.md, so they grow with that set: more signs and signers before any result from the app means much.

5. **The app in Swedish**: every string the learner reads, in `frontend/` and in the notes the API sends back (`takk/practice.py`). Hardcoded Swedish, no translation framework, because the app is for practising TAKK and has no other audience. The reasons a recording cannot be used come from `checks.py` in the verification package, which the English collection app shares, so the wording moved into a `notes` argument that each app passes (its English `NOTES` stay the default, and the recordings it has already stored keep their wording). — done, checked with `uv run pytest`, `npm run check` and `npm test`, and the user has since used the Swedish page in their own browser (2026-09-21).

6. **Word sets**: a handful of categories a beginner actually starts from (mat, kläder, familj, känslor, vardagsrutiner, ...), each a list of lexicon ids, as one data file in the repository. TAKK practice needs on the order of a hundred signs, not the lexicon's 21,680, and a set is what onboarding, recommendation and a session are all picked from, so nothing above this step can be built before it. — done. The categories are the lexicon's own (user, 2026-09-20): Svenskt teckenspråkslexikon sorts its entries into Swedish subject categories (Djur, Kläder, Mat och dryck, ...) at https://teckensprakslexikon.su.se/kategori, and every entry page names the ones it belongs to as a path ("Sport > klubbar och föreningar > NHL"). They are read off the entry pages by `parse_entry`, and `takk/vocabulary.py` maps a category's lexicon ids to the glossary's signs, by the top level of the path or by the whole path (an id is the clip id, and entries sharing a sign form collapse to one sign). No LLM is involved; an earlier attempt to have one invent the words is in the history (`f64a475`), as is a crawl of the category listing pages (`22cbc92`), which was dropped when the listings turned out to be incomplete. A category is offered to the learner as a pack of step 9, as large as the lexicon makes it, except Österberg 1916, whose historical forms are never taught, and it is ordered by how often the lexicon counts its words (`category_words`, see the findings), so a beginner meets the everyday ones first.

7. **Starter packs** (`takk/vocabulary.py`, `takk/packs.json`): a pack is a short list of everyday Swedish words, resolved to lexicon entries at load rather than written as ids, so the list stays readable and a lexicon that has moved an entry fails loudly instead of teaching a wrong sign. `Första tecknen` (15 words: mamma, pappa, hej, tack, ja, nej, mer, klar, äta, dricka, mjölk, vatten, sova, toalett, hjälp) is the first one. — done, resolved against the current crawl and unit tested; not wired into the app yet. The lexicon's own categories turned out to be no help for a beginner (see findings), which is why the packs exist.

8. **Progress and spaced repetition in the browser** (`frontend/src/lib/progress.ts`): per sign a Leitner box and a next-due date in `localStorage`, moved up by an accepted attempt and back to the first box by a rejected one. The four boxes are due again after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days (user, 2026-09-21), the whole schedule being the `DAYS` array. A word moves only when its fate in a pass is settled: it moves up a box once it has been accepted `accepts` times in that pass, two by default and adjustable in the page, and back to the first box the moment it is missed (user, 2026-09-21). A word the pass missed stays in the first box when it finishes, rather than climbing back out of it: moving it up would mean that missing a word promoted it further than signing it right the first time, since the miss had already put it in box 1 for the finish to count from. Box 1 therefore means "new or missed today", and a completed pass leaves in it exactly the words it taught for the first time. An accepted attempt that still leaves it short of that number stores nothing at all, so a pass left half-finished does not leave words sitting half-learned in a box. A pass always demotes but promotes only what was due (user, 2026-09-21, asked when step 11 was designed): a correct answer on a sign that is not yet due moves neither its box nor its date, only its `seen`. A box is a claim about an interval — the third means "still remembered after seven days" — and an answer on the second day has not tested that, so promoting would push the next repetition out to an interval that was never met, and a word could climb out of the boxes by being drilled in one sitting; it is why Anki does not reschedule "review ahead" by default. A miss is evidence whatever the day, since forgetting a sign that was not due means its interval was already too long, so it always falls back to the first box. The asymmetry means an extra pass can only repair the boxes, never inflate them. New words are taken from the chosen packs in turn rather than pack after pack, so choosing Djur and Mat och dryck does not mean a month of animals first. `seen` records when a sign was last practised and `first` when it was met, which is what the day's counts need — a new word and a missed old one both sit in the first box, and the card is stored beside it — its word and its lexicon entry: a box belongs to a sign class, and a class has many words, so "svart" showed up as "Oden" on the progress page until the word practised was the one kept. The entry is kept for the same reason and so that practising a word again looks nothing up in the packs (user, 2026-09-21), which come from the crawl while the boxes come from `localStorage`: an entry dropped from every pack would otherwise lose the form description its card shows. A box written before the entry was stored is given one the next time its word is practised. No accounts, no database and no user state on the server for as long as this holds. "Mina tecken" (`frontend/src/routes/framsteg/+page.svelte`) shows the boxes: the counts per box and every practised sign with its box, its next repetition and the packs it is in. — done, unit tested (`progress.test.ts`); the store is read and written by the session of step 9. Open: export and import of the file for a second device, and a recording that could not be used is currently neither an answer nor a repetition.


9. **A practice session**: "dagens pass" (`frontend/src/routes/pass/+page.svelte`), `size` words, five by default and adjustable in the page, the due signs of step 8 first and words never practised filling up whatever room the repetitions leave (user, 2026-09-21). The pass is a queue rather than a list: a word that has not yet been accepted `accepts` times goes back to the end of it, so the pass cycles until every one of its words is finished, and a missed word is met again the same pass. The very first time a word is met it is taught with its lexicon clip; every later turn, in this pass or a later one, is a flash card: the word alone, the clip only after the attempt is scored, since showing it first makes the session recognition rather than recall (user, 2026-09-20). Looking the sign up is offered and keeps it in the first box whatever the model says. A card shows the lexicon's own description of the sign's form next to the clip (`GET /api/form/{entry}`, `vocabulary.sign_forms`): it is the only teaching text the lexicon publishes, it is on all but 30 of the entries with a video, and without it the teach step is imitation of a clip and nothing else. The verdict is told the word that was asked for, since the sign that scores it is named after its class ("grön" is scored as `sts:land-00416`, which read as "Rätt: det var land" until the page said so outright). The choice is a pure function (`session` in `progress.ts`) over the starter packs of step 7, which the API serves from `GET /api/packs` (`starter_packs` resolved once at startup against the served glossary, so a word the glossary cannot sign fails the server rather than the learner). This is the point at which the app stops being a single screen: the page is now "Dagens pass" and "Fri övning". The learner chooses what the session draws from (user, 2026-09-20): a pack is either a starter pack or one of the lexicon's categories and it does not matter which, so `vocabulary.packs` serves both as one shape and the page turns them on and off, with the choice in `localStorage` next to the boxes. — done, and the user has run passes in their own browser (2026-09-21) and confirmed the pass and its boxes work, which is also what turned up the box mistakes in the findings. The new words of a category come in the order of step 6's `category_words`, so the ones a session draws are the everyday ones.

10. **Sentences written by an LLM** (the first use of an LLM API in the project, `takk/sentences.py`, `POST /api/sentence`): a practice turn of several signs is given a short Swedish sentence to speak while signing them. — done, unit tested with a stubbed client, and the user has practised from written sentences in their own browser (2026-09-21).

    The words to practise are the words on the cards, not the names of the signs that score them (see the findings): a learner practising "blå" is scored by `sts:öga-02636`, and both the sentence and the alignment use "blå". `POST /api/attempt` therefore takes a `spoken` word per sign, defaulting to the sign's own name for the free practice page, where the sign's name is what is shown. The words are given to the model, so it only writes around them, and the contract is that it uses each of them **exactly once and exactly as written** — no inflection (user, 2026-09-21). The reason is that the signs are found in the recording by timing those words in the audio, so a form that was not said as written scores below `MIN_WORD_SCORE` and the sentence is refused; forbidding inflection also makes the whole answer checkable (every word a whole token, once) instead of trusting the model to report which form it chose, and the order the signs are made in is then read off the sentence rather than reported. Swedish gives this up cheaply, since the infinitive is the dictionary form ("Jag vill äta") and a noun sits in its base form behind an article ("en hund"). Allowing inflection was designed and rejected in the same conversation; if the sentences turn out stilted, the model returning `{word, spoken}` pairs is the change to make, and it is the same size then as now. A sentence that breaks the contract is asked for again once, with what was wrong, and after that the turn falls back to practising its words one at a time. Nothing about the learner's vocabulary is sent: only the key words are signed, so the rest of the sentence can be any Swedish.

    Every turn is spoken, with no exceptions (user, 2026-09-21): a sentence of two or three words where the queue offers them, a sentence of one key word where it does not, and the word said on its own when a word is being taught for the first time or no sentence could be written. `POST /api/attempt` therefore always requires the microphone and always locates the signs by the words heard in the audio, which removed the `spoken` flag, the `split` field and the whole-recording branch rather than adding anything. An exception for the word being taught was built first, on the grounds that a new word is copied from its clip rather than recalled and that scoring the whole recording is more forgiving; the user rejected it because a learner should never have to remember which kind of turn they are in. A turn is built from the queue by `progress.turn`: the head, and while the head is a word that may be combined (box 2 and up), every later word that may be too. Which of them the sentence uses is the model's choice (user, 2026-09-21), because it is what knows which words make one sentence; only the head has to appear, so the turn still practises what the pass scheduled next, and at most 3 words are signed in one recording. Choosing the combination here first produced sentences that mentioned each word in turn rather than being about anything (see the findings), and the words the sentence used are read back off it by a whole-word search rather than reported, so the model has nothing to be trusted about. The first box holds exactly the new and the missed words, neither of which belongs in a sentence. Each sign's verdict is an ordinary answer for its word, so a miss inside a sentence sends that word back to the first box and it returns alone; a recording that could not be split is no answer for any of them. The model is `claude-sonnet-5` (`--sentence-model`), asked once per turn, and its client is checked at startup: without credentials the app says so and every turn is one sign, which is the path that needs no model at all.

11. **Repetera allt du kan** (`frontend/src/routes/pass/+page.svelte?alla=1`, `known` in `progress.ts`): the same pass, drawn from everything already practised instead of from what is due and what is new. It exists because "dagens pass" runs dry — nothing due and the chosen packs exhausted — and because the learner may simply want to go through what they know. Nothing new is taught in it: the pool is the boxes of step 8, so vocabulary still grows only through dagens pass, and the two modes divide cleanly. It is reached from "Mina tecken" and from a finished pass. — done, unit tested (`progress.test.ts`), checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`.

    The words are drawn without replacement and weighted by `1 / DAYS[box - 1]`, so a word in the first box is twenty-one times as likely as one in the last (user, 2026-09-21). The weight is the rate the ordinary schedule would meet the word at, which makes a review pass that schedule run early and spends its turns on the weakest words. Ordering by the longest unseen was the first proposal and is exactly wrong: the last box is due in three weeks, so its words are always the ones longest unseen, and the pass would drill what is already strongest. Covering every word is not this mode's job either, since every word comes up on its own due date anyway.

12. **Free signing scored against what was said**: instead of a given sentence, the learner speaks and signs freely, and the app reports how many of the key words were signed. The speech model already loaded by `takk/speech.py` is a CTC model, so decoding its emissions gives the transcript; the content words of that transcript that exist in the lexicon become the expected signs, and the existing forced alignment and `split_signs` scoring run unchanged. — planned

13. **Conversation mode** (user, 2026-09-20): a chat with an LLM in which the learner answers by speaking and signing, and gets their signing corrected turn by turn. Two ways to run a turn, and the mode should support both: *guided*, where the LLM writes the sentence to speak and sign (step 10) and the app scores it sign by sign as today, and *free*, where the LLM asks something and the learner answers in their own words, scored by step 12. Guided is the easier and the stricter of the two; free is the one that resembles using TAKK. The LLM sees which signs were accepted and rejected, so it can bring a missed sign back in its next turn, which is spaced repetition inside a conversation. — planned

14. **Onboarding as a chat**: an LLM asks about previous experience, already known signs, the reason for learning and the everyday context (barn, vård, förskola, ...) and turns the answers into chosen sets of step 6 and a starting state for step 8. It can only choose from sets that exist, so it follows step 6, not precedes it. — planned

15. **State on the server**: accounts and a database, when the browser's own storage of step 8 actually gets in the way (a second device, or more than one learner). Not before: a single table reached through a login is the kind of thing that is cheap to add late and expensive to carry early. — planned

## Decisions

- **The app is in Swedish, and keeps the learner's state in the browser until something needs more** (user, 2026-09-20). The learning experience is planned as steps 5 to 13 above: word sets and a practice session first, with no LLM and no server state, then the LLM steps (sentences, free signing, conversation, onboarding), and accounts and a database only when the browser's storage gets in the way. The LLM is also the point of the exercise and not only a means, as the user wants the app to be an occasion to use LLM APIs.

- **The model picks which of the turn's words to use, and uses them uninflected** (user, 2026-09-21). The words of the turn are offered to it with only the first required, since deciding the combination in the app produced sentences that listed words instead of saying something. See step 10 for why inflection is forbidden and what it would take to allow it.

- **Everything is spoken** (user, 2026-09-21). TAKK is signed while speaking, so every attempt is said aloud however few of its words are signed, and the microphone is required throughout. A word being taught for the first time is shown with its lexicon clip as before, and is said on its own while being signed. The rule is uniform because a learner should not have to remember which kind of turn they are in, which is worth more than the two arguments for exempting the teaching turn (a clip is copied rather than recalled, and whole-recording scoring is more forgiving than alignment).

- **A pass always demotes, but promotes only a sign that was due** (user, 2026-09-21). See step 8 for why an early correct answer is no evidence about the interval its box claims, and step 11 for the review mode that rests on it.

- **The categories of step 6 are the lexicon's own, and the sentences of step 10 are written by an LLM per request** (user, 2026-09-20). The lexicon publishes subject categories and names them on every entry page, so they are crawled rather than generated: they are authoritative, free, and already tied to lexicon ids. An LLM was going to label the signs instead, on the mistaken assumption that the lexicon had no categories; look at what a source already publishes before generating it. The crawl takes the whole entry page in one pass (user, 2026-09-20: download everything about the dataset once, rather than adding a second file per field), so `parse_entry` now also reads the entry's other wording, its English translation and its hit counts in the lexicon, the corpus and the surveys. A sentence is a different matter: it depends on what this learner already knows and what is due for practice, which is only known when the request arrives, so it is written per request. The consequence is that a deployed API needs an API key for the sentences, and that the categories cost nothing at all.

- **The frontend is a SvelteKit app, built and hosted apart from the API** (user decision, 2026-09-20: a real learning app is coming, with lesson flow, progress and phone use, and a Node build step is acceptable). `frontend/` holds it (SvelteKit 2, Svelte 5, TypeScript, Vite, `adapter-static`, prerendered with `ssr = false`), outside the Python package so that `node_modules` and the build never reach the wheel. The API does not serve it: Vite's dev and preview servers proxy `/api` to `127.0.0.1:8002`, so only one port is forwarded from this machine, and a deployed build reaches the API through `VITE_API_BASE`. The user rejected serving the built files from FastAPI as coupling that hurts deployment and availability.

- **The large unchanging files the browser downloads ("media") are meant for a static host, not the API process**: `holistic_landmarker.task` (13.7 MB, `/api/model`) and the lexicon clips (17 GB in `data/raw/sts-lexikon/movies`, `/api/reference/<clip id>`). They are read off disk, while `POST /api/attempt` is what needs torch, the checkpoint and the ~45 s startup that embeds the lexicon. Nothing moves yet; the design rule is that the API hands the frontend URLs for clips and the model rather than paths it assembles itself, so they can be repointed at a CDN server-side. The goal is that searching the lexicon and watching clips keep working while the scoring API is asleep.

- **The app applies the threshold, it does not choose it.** What an attempt has to reach is decided from the model's numbers, in the decisions of ROADMAP.md (a provisional 0.38, `--threshold`).

- **Judging a sentence sign by sign while it is signed was built and then removed** (user, 2026-09-20). It worked: a verdict appeared about one sign behind the signer, from a rest or from the next key word being spoken. The commits are in the history (reverted in `a0d3e0a` and the three before it) if it is ever wanted again.

## Findings

- **Imposing the combination made the sentences worse than offering it** (user, 2026-09-21). Asked for a sentence containing both "röd" and "blå", the model wrote "Nallen har en röd napp och ett öga." — two objects invented to carry the two colours, which is a list with grammar rather than a sentence about anything. The words of a turn are now offered with only the head required, so the same pair can produce "Har du en röd tröja?" and blå waits for a turn it fits; the user confirmed the sentences are usable that way (2026-09-21). The set the sentence actually used is read off it, which makes the validation smaller than before rather than larger: the head must be there, no word twice, at most three in all.

- **A sentence was written about the sign's name instead of the word being practised** (user, 2026-09-21, found on the first sentence they were given). Asked to practise "röd" and "blå", the model was sent `["röd", "öga"]` and wrote "Nallen har en röd napp och ett öga.": `spoken_word` had turned each sign into its class name, and "blå" is scored by `sts:öga-02636` because blå and öga are one sign form and the lower entry names the class. The sentence passed validation, since "öga" really was in it, but nothing was in bold for the second sign, the alignment listened for "öga" while the learner signed blå, and the verdict was "Inga händer syntes". The separation between the word on a card and the sign that scores it was already known and handled on the flash cards and the progress page; the sentence was the third place that needed it. Both the sentence and the attempt now carry the learner's own word.

- **Forced alignment never fails, so a sentence signed in silence was scored as if it had been spoken** (user, 2026-09-21, found by testing the speech-only split). `torchaudio.functional.forced_align` is a Viterbi path: it returns an alignment for any audio, and the wildcard token makes one cheap to find, so silence gives each key word a narrow span somewhere and `split_speech` cuts between them. The user signed two signs without speaking and got two verdicts, both accepted. The alignment's own score is what tells the cases apart: aligned against silence or room noise the words score -5.4 to -3.9 (measured on `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish` with "hej" and "mamma"), while a word actually spoken scores near 0. `MIN_WORD_SCORE` is -3.0, between the two, and a sentence with a word below it is refused and names the word. The user confirmed in their own browser that a normally spoken sentence passes it and a silent one does not (2026-09-21). The threshold is provisional in the same way as the acceptance threshold of ROADMAP.md: mumbling and a noisy room have not been measured, so the server prints the scores of every refused sentence for as long as that holds.

- **A word could reach the third box in a single pass** (user, 2026-09-21, found by running a pass and reading "Mina tecken"). Two mistakes in the first version of step 9 let it. A sign was put back in the pass whenever it was in the first box after an answer, which was meant to catch a missed sign but also caught a *new* word answered correctly, since a first accept moves a word from no box to box 1 — so every new word was asked twice. And the record button stayed enabled after a verdict, so recording the same card again counted as a second answer and moved the box again. Three accepts in one pass is box 3, which is due in three days, while a word answered once sat in box 1, due the next day. The fix is the rule the user asked for instead: the box moves once per pass, when the word reaches its accepts, and a second recording of the same card is a retry that shows a verdict and changes nothing.

- **What a category should teach first is `lexicon_hits`, and the word to show is not the sign's name** (2026-09-20). Of the three counts the entry pages publish, `lexicon_hits` is on every entry and its head is what TAKK teaches (Djur: hund, katt, djur; Kläder: kläder, sko, tröja; Sport: träna, fotboll, ishockey). `corpus_hits` exists for only 6,003 of 21,692 entries and ranks by STS corpus frequency, and `survey_hits` is close to an anti-signal: the surveys ask about rare signs, so it puts avocado and hamster first. Ordering alone was not enough, because a category's words were the names of sign classes, which are labelled by their lowest entry: "Djur" began at "hane" and "batteri" and Sport at "finns" and "din", and the class also imported those words' counts. A category's word is therefore taken from the entry that is in the category, with the class kept as the sign that scores it — the same separation the starter packs already needed. Two entries of one form in one category become one word, and so do two forms written the same way ("fotboll" is signed in two ways), which as a flash card would be scored against whichever form was drawn. The lexicon's 14,343 category memberships come out as 9,887 words.

- **The lexicon's categories are subject areas, not a learning order**, and two traps sit in the words (2026-09-20). By size they are Sport 2,238 signs, Geografi 1,524, Sjukvård 1,136, Egennamn 757, and only then Barn 320, Kläder 131, Känslor 92, Familj 74, Hälsningsfras 14, so a beginner cannot start from them; 11,877 of the glossary's 16,026 signs have at least one. What a beginner needs is covered, though: 38 of 39 words of a typical TAKK starter vocabulary resolve to a lexicon sign (only "godnatt" is missing). The first trap is that the word to practise is not the label of the sign that scores it — "äta" is scored as `sts:livsmedel-01265`, because entries of one sign form are one class labelled by its lowest entry — so a pack keeps the word, the lexicon id and the sign apart. The second is Österberg's 1916 dictionary: 417 entries of historical sign forms, in the category "Österberg 1916", which a pack must not teach. "hjälp" resolved into one of them until a heading was made to beat another entry's `also` wording.

- Splitting a recording of several signs at the rests (`split_signs`, `MIN_REST` 0.4 s) on the 51 Swedish browser recordings: every recording is one sign, and every one of the 1,275 pairs joined end to end splits into exactly two. The margin is thin but real: inside a sign the hands are lost for at most 0.23 s, while the rest between two joined recordings (one's tail plus the next's lead) is at least 0.43 s, and deliberately lowering the hands between signs takes longer. The user confirmed the sentence mode works in their own browser (2026-09-19).

- Timing a TAKK sentence's spoken words instead of splitting at the rests (`takk/speech.py`): forced alignment needs the words, not a transcript, which is exactly what a sentence of lexicon signs already is, so no speech recognition is involved. KBLab's `easyaligner` (https://github.com/kb-labb/easyaligner, MIT) is the same recipe packaged, and the wildcard token is taken from it, but it was not adopted: it pins `torch>=2.7,<2.9` against this project's 2.14, and it is built for batch alignment of long recordings over directories of files, with `pyannote-audio` and `silero-vad` that its own documentation says alignment does not use. Its core is a thin wrapper over `torchaudio.functional.forced_align`, so only that is used here, plus the wildcard applied between the words rather than only at the ends of the transcript. A wildcard column of zeros is the highest possible log probability, so the alignment gives each key word close to the fewest frames it can and the wildcards everything else; the words therefore come out as narrow spans at their clearest frames, which is enough to cut between them. `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish` is 1.2 GB, loads in 2.8 s and aligns a 4 s recording in 0.4 s on the CPU. The user confirmed on real speech in their own browser that the words split the sentence correctly (2026-09-19).
