# Roadmap — the TAKK practice app

Status of the app and the plan ahead, as ROADMAP.md holds them for the isolated sign verification work the app is built on (`src/isolated_sign_validation/`). Update this file when a step is finished, a decision is made, or the plan changes. Read the roadmap of the package you touch, both when a change crosses. See README.md for how to run the app.

The app (user, 2026-09-19): practising TAKK (tecken som alternativ och kompletterande kommunikation), from single signs to sentences. TAKK signs the key words of spoken Swedish with STS lexicon signs, in Swedish word order and closer to isolation than fluent STS, so a sentence is a known sequence of lexicon signs to find and score in order. What remains is splitting the recording into signs, possibly slightly blended forms, and errors adding up over a sentence's signs.

## The shape of the app

The user drew the app as a screen map on 2026-09-21, and it is what the steps below build towards. A menu offers three sections and nothing else:

- **Sök** (`/sök`) — finding signs and choosing which ones to learn. A search field ("Sök efter ord eller teman") or a camera ("Sök med tecken") produces a list of signs, each row a checkbox, the word and a video preview, with "Lägg till alla" above it. Ticking a sign adds it to the learner's vocabulary.
- **Träna** (`/träna`) — a camera preview with framing feedback and two ways on: **Nya ord**, a card per word with the lexicon's reference clip beside the camera, looping to the next word, and **Repetera**, a connected text read line by line over the words already learned.
- **Tecken** (`/tecken`) — the learner's own signs: a bar chart of how they are spread over the repetition schedule (nya, imorgon, 3 dagar, 7 dagar, 21 dagar), a line chart of signs met over time, and "Nollställ".

The first section is named for what the learner does there and the third for what it holds (user, 2026-09-21). The two were briefly the other way round, as "Tecken" and "Framsteg"; "Tecken" belongs to the learner's own signs, and the way in is a search.

## Steps

Steps 1 to 7 are built and in use. Steps 8 to 14 are the blueprint above and are the work ahead, in the order they are numbered: each one is useful on its own, and the state the app keeps grows only when a step needs it. Throughout, the app's numbers rest on the self-recorded Swedish clips of step 10 of ROADMAP.md, so they grow with that set: more signs and signers before any result from the app means much.

1. **Live landmarks in the browser** (`frontend/src/lib/tracking.ts`, `landmarks.ts`): the browser tracks the pose with the MediaPipe Tasks HolisticLandmarker on the GPU delegate, fully live (user, 2026-09-19) — VIDEO mode on every camera frame it keeps up with, skipping the frames that arrive while it is busy, drawn over the camera image, with the rate it reaches shown (a warning below 20 fps). A recording keeps each result with its capture time and is resampled to 30 fps, the latest tracked frame at each step, as the collection app's transcode picks frames. — done. Open: the real frame rate on the user's GPU, and whether results hold up when frames are skipped.

2. **Scoring an attempt** (`takk/practice.py`, `POST /api/attempt`): the server checks the recording, prepares it (mirrored by the attempt's dominant hand, or the user's stated handedness for two-handed attempts), embeds it with `iv14_h384_e20` and accepts when the mean cosine similarity to the sign's lexicon clips reaches the threshold (see decisions); it also returns the closest sign of the whole lexicon, which step 10 needs. The lexicon's embeddings are cached next to the run and take about 45 s to build at startup. Nothing is stored, there are no accounts and no user state on the server. — done, checked end to end in headless Chrome (see findings).

3. **Splitting a recording by the words that were spoken** (`takk/speech.py`): TAKK is spoken while signed, so the words are known and nothing is recognized (user, 2026-09-19). The browser records the microphone alongside the landmarks and `torchaudio.functional.forced_align` over `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish` times the key words in the audio, cutting halfway from one word's end to the next word's start, with a wildcard token between and around them absorbing the rest of the sentence so the signer speaks naturally. The outer edges sit `PRE_ROLL` (0.5 s) before the first word and `POST_ROLL` (0.4 s) after the last, so what is recorded before the signer begins and after they finish is not scored as part of the first and last signs. Splitting at the rests instead was built first and removed (see findings). Each word carries its alignment score and an attempt whose words were not heard is refused, or scored as a miss when the caller passes `unheard_is_miss` — the story does, since there the recording is not worth remaking. — done; the user confirmed in their own browser that a spoken sentence scores sign by sign, the same sentence signed in silence is refused, and a word said but not signed is a miss with a verdict of its own (2026-09-21).

4. **The recording begins and ends with the voice** (user, 2026-09-21), so nothing is pressed: `frontend/src/lib/voice.ts` reads the loudness of the newest 21 ms from an `AnalyserNode` on the microphone that is already open, fifty times a second, and `Recorder.svelte` arms a recording when the card appears, starts the attempt after three readings above the threshold and ends it after twenty-four under it. The quiet room is measured for 0.7 s first, since absolute levels mean nothing across microphones, and the threshold is three times that floor; a reading has to fall to six tenths of the threshold to count as quiet, so a soft last syllable does not cut the attempt short. Waiting armed for ten seconds throws the recording away and starts a new one, so a long wait is never sent. The reading is deliberately crude and that is the design: a window opened by a door has no spoken words in it, so alignment scores them under `MIN_WORD_SCORE` and the attempt is refused — a false start costs one window and never a wrong verdict, which is what pays for having no voice detector in the browser. Recording runs from when the card appears rather than from the first word, because the hands rise before the voice. — done, practised with in the user's own browser (2026-09-21). Every number is a knob and none has been measured.

5. **A SvelteKit app in Swedish** (`frontend/`, see the decisions): the camera, the landmarker loop, the common landmark layout and the API calls live in typed modules (`src/lib/`) that the components only render. Every string the learner reads is hardcoded Swedish, with no translation framework, because the app is for practising TAKK and has no other audience; the reasons a recording cannot be used come from `checks.py` in the verification package, which the English collection app shares, so the wording is a `notes` argument each app passes. `takk` is the API alone and never serves the page. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test`, `npm run build` and `uv run pytest`, and in the user's own browser (2026-09-21). Open: CORS on the API (`allow_origin`), needed the first time the page is served from another origin than the API.

6. **Progress and spaced repetition in the browser** (`frontend/src/lib/progress.ts`): per sign a Leitner box and a next-due date in `localStorage`. The four boxes are due again after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days (user, 2026-09-21), the whole schedule being the `DAYS` array. A miss drops a sign to the first box at once; an accept promotes it only if it was due (see decisions). `seen` records when a sign was last practised and `first` when it was met, which is what the charts of step 14 need. The card is stored beside the box — its word and its lexicon entry — because a box belongs to a sign class and a class has many words ("svart" showed up as "Oden" until the word practised was the one kept), and because practising a word again should look nothing up (user, 2026-09-21). — done, unit tested (`progress.test.ts`). Open: export and import of the store for a second device, and a recording that could not be used is currently neither an answer nor a repetition.

7. **Nya ord and Sagan**, the two modes the app has (user, 2026-09-21): new words are taught on cards (`frontend/src/routes/+page.svelte`) with the lexicon clip and the lexicon's own description of the sign's form (`GET /api/form/{entry}`, on all but 30 of the entries with a video), and everything else is repetition inside one Swedish story (`takk/story.py`, `POST /api/story`, `frontend/src/routes/saga/+page.svelte`) written over the words the learner already knows. A card is one word and nothing else, since the word is being taught rather than used, and the verdict says only "Rätt" or "Fel", named by the word that was asked for rather than the sign that scores it. Nothing is pressed between cards: a word signed right is recorded and the next card comes at once, a word missed or a recording that could not be used is simply armed again with the clip still on screen. The story's words are drawn without replacement and weighted by `1 / DAYS[box - 1]`, so a word in the first box is twenty-one times as likely as one in the last — that weight is the rate the ordinary schedule would meet the word at, so a story spends its words on the weakest ones. The daily pass, the review pass and free practice were all removed along the way; what they established is in the decisions and findings below. — done as far as it goes; the story is a prototype and step 13 rebuilds its page.

8. **Menyn** (`frontend/src/routes/+page.svelte`): the sections of the blueprint and nothing else, in place of the nav bar that used to sit above every page. Every page but the menu carries one way back (user, 2026-09-21), so nothing depends on the browser's own back button; it goes to the section above rather than to wherever the learner came from, since the map is a tree and a page's parent is its path without the last segment.

    **The menu asks which hand the learner signs with, once** (user, 2026-09-21), and `frontend/src/lib/hand.ts` keeps the answer in `localStorage`. Every recording is mirrored by it, so both camera screens used to carry a radio for something that never changes between attempts; they now read the stored answer and show nothing. It can be changed again under Tecken, beside "Nollställ", which is the one place the learner's own settings live. The pages moved to where the map puts them — the Nya ord cards to `/träna/nya`, the story from `/saga` to `/träna/repetera`, and `/träna` is their hub — and the third section stayed where it was. The pack picker is still on the Nya ord page and goes in step 9, which is when the app has another source of words; leaving it until then keeps the app usable between the steps. **Träna is only its two links for now**: the camera preview and the framing feedback the map draws on it belong to steps 11 and 12, which is also when the camera starts opening once for both modes. Sök is missing from the menu until step 9 makes it something to open. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build` (the routes with "ä" in them build and serve as `/tr%C3%A4na`). Not tried in a browser yet.

9. **Sök: choosing the vocabulary by searching the lexicon** (user, 2026-09-21). The search field takes a word or a theme and returns a list of signs, each row a checkbox and a video preview, with "Lägg till alla" above it; ticking adds the sign to the learner's vocabulary. This **replaces the packs** as the way vocabulary grows: `takk/packs.json`, `GET /api/packs`, the pack picker and `chosenWords`, `loadChosen` and `saveChosen` go, and `known` and `boxes` read the progress store alone. The lexicon's categories stay, as what a theme search matches: typing "mat" returns Mat och dryck ordered by `lexicon_hits` (see findings), so the everyday words come first. The one piece of new state is a sign that has been chosen but never practised — "nya" in the charts of step 14 — since a sign used to enter the store only when it was first accepted; `add` writes a box-0 entry with the word, the entry and the time it was picked, `fresh` is what Nya ord teaches from, and `record` is unchanged. Box 0 has no due date, and `known` skips it, so a sign is never repeated before it has been taught. Unticking a sign removes it again, unless it has been practised: its checkbox is then locked, since the tick is not what holds the learning. A result's clip plays by itself, muted and looping, rather than waiting to be pressed, since the point of the row is to see the sign.

    **The store has no backwards compatibility** (user, 2026-09-21: "you don't have to assume that we should store any old word state. Let's start clean"). `Learned` now requires the box, the due date, the time it was picked, the word and the lexicon entry, and the fallbacks that stood in for a record written by an older version are gone — a sign the search did not name is named after the sign itself when it is picked, so the store always holds the word to show. A store written before this change is cleared with "Nollställ". — done, checked with `uv run pytest`, `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`; the search is unit tested against a small crawl (`tests/takk/test_vocabulary.py`) and the boxes against the new box 0 (`progress.test.ts`). Not tried in a browser yet, and the search has not been run against the real 21,680-entry crawl.

    Nya ord moved to the picked words in the same step rather than in step 12, because removing the packs leaves it with no other source. It takes the five longest waiting and says to go to Tecken when there are none.

10. **Sök med tecken** (`POST /api/search`): the camera records landmarks only — no microphone, because this is a lookup and not an attempt — and the server answers with the `CLOSEST` (20) lexicon signs nearest by cosine similarity, into the same rows the text search fills. The ranking is the one `judge` already computes over the whole glossary, and the recording is checked and prepared exactly as an attempt is, mirrored by the dominant hand; a recording the checks refuse answers with no rows and the reason. A sign is offered under the word it is best known by (`Index.by_sign`, the most counted word of that form), so a row from the camera is the same shape as a row from the field. — done, checked with `uv run pytest`, `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`; the endpoint is tested by ranking two signs and by a recording with no hands in it. Not tried in a browser yet.

    The camera is **a second state of the Sök page** rather than a page of its own, though the map draws it as one: the two searches fill the same list, so a route would have to hand the rows back to it. The map's flow is what is kept — field or camera, then the rows. The recording is started and stopped by hand ("Starta"/"Stopp"), unlike an attempt, which the voice begins and ends: there is nothing being said to begin it with.

    **The recording stays in the camera's own place** (user, 2026-09-21), as the searched word stays in the field: the tracked landmarks of the lookup replay on a loop over the live picture, in the same box and at the same size, with the rows under it. A search never switches the page back to the field either — the learner chose the camera, so it stays until they press "Sök med ord". Only the landmarks are kept and replayed, never the camera's picture, which is also all that was sent; `draw` takes a size rather than the video element it used to read one from. A word search clears the replay, since the rows are then an answer to the word, and so does the next recording.

    **The camera closes when the component goes away** (found while sharing it, 2026-09-22). `Tracker` had no teardown at all: leaving a page left its `MediaStream` tracks open, so the camera light stayed on and the next page opened a second stream beside it. `Tracker.stop()` now stops every track, drops the video's source, closes the audio context and the landmarker, and lets the frame loop end; the component calls it when it unmounts.

    Once opened, the camera stays in the page and is only hidden (user, 2026-09-21: the second lookup showed a black picture). `Tracker` holds the video element it was started with and runs its loop on that element's `requestVideoFrameCallback`, so leaving the camera unmounted it and left the tracker driving a detached element while the remounted one had no stream. The same applies wherever the camera is shown and hidden, which step 12 does for both practice modes.

11. **Framing feedback under the camera** (`frontend/src/lib/framing.ts`): both camera screens say how the signer sits, read five times a second off the frame being tracked right now, so the framing is fixed before a recording is spent on it. One frame, a handful of distances and no history — `checks.py` still has the last word on a finished recording. It asks, in this order, for both shoulders, for a distance that keeps the shoulders between `NARROW` (0.16) and `WIDE` (0.55) of the frame width, for the signer to be within `OFF_CENTRE` (0.18) of the middle, and for `HEADROOM` (0.08) of the frame above the head, where a good many signs are made; while recording it also asks for the hands, which are only expected to be up then. Every number is a guess read off the lexicon's own framing and every one is a knob. **It is silent when the framing is right** (user, 2026-09-21): `framing` returns the empty string, and what is wrong is shown over the bottom of the picture it is about rather than as a line under it — unmirrored, since the view itself is flipped like a mirror. **A complaint also has to hold `PATIENCE` (5) readings, about a second, before it is shown** (user, 2026-09-22): hands come down the moment a sign ends, so the camera used to complain about the pause between signs. `settle` keeps the count, a framing that is right clears the note at once rather than after a wait, and a new complaint leaves the old one up until it has held, so they do not flicker. — done, unit tested on synthetic frames (`framing.test.ts`). Not tried in a browser yet.

    **The frame rate is no longer shown** (user, 2026-09-21), and with it went `Tracker`'s `onRate` callback, its `delegate` and the warning below 20 fps. The framing is what a learner can act on; the rate was a developer's number. The open question of step 1 — what the rate actually is on this GPU — now needs a measurement rather than a glance at the page.

12. **Träna as a hub** (`frontend/src/routes/träna/+layout.svelte`): the camera preview with its framing feedback above the two ways on, Nya ord and Repetera. The camera belongs to the **section's layout**, which SvelteKit keeps mounted while the pages under it come and go, so it opens once on the hub and is still running when either mode starts — and the element the tracker draws on is never taken out from under it, which is what black-screened the sign search before it was hidden rather than unmounted. `Camera` (`src/lib/camera.svelte.ts`) is what the layout puts in context: the tracker, the video element, the lexicon, the framing note, the handedness and whether a recording is running. The picture is `Camera.svelte`, which both places that show a camera mount (user, 2026-09-22): it owns the video, the canvas the landmarks are drawn on, the startup, the framing loop and the note over the bottom of the picture, and a page that needs something else in the picture — Sök's replay of the sign it searched with — passes it as children. `Recorder.svelte` is only the recording now — the voice, the timer and the scoring — and no longer opens a camera, holds a lexicon or draws a picture; the handedness radio moved to the layout, where it belongs to the camera rather than to an attempt. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. Not tried in a browser yet.

    **Nya ord shows its clip under the camera rather than beside it**, which the map draws side by side. The camera is one element above the page, and putting the page's content beside it would mean the layout laying out what it does not know. It is a CSS change in the layout if it turns out to matter.

13. **The story is read line by line** (user, 2026-09-21): `POST /api/story` writes `LINES` (6) lines in one request, and the page shows a window of them — `CONTEXT` (1) line above and one below in grey, only the line to sign now solid, with its signed words marked and coloured by their verdicts. There is no end screen and no summary: when the learner reaches the last written line the next six are asked for and appended, so the story goes on for as long as they keep signing. A writer that failed is not asked again by itself, since a dead server would otherwise be asked forever; the page offers "Skriv fortsättningen" instead. `MOST_WORDS` (3 signed words per line) and the contract that each word appears exactly once and exactly as written stay as they are, and the verdicts are kept per line so an earlier line keeps its colours as it greys out. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. Not tried in a browser yet; `LINES` and `CONTEXT` are guesses and are knobs.

    **The mode is not called anything, and explains nothing** (user, 2026-09-21: "I don't want to call it 'saga' anywhere, this is just how you repeat words"). "Sagan" is gone from every page, and so is the paragraph that told the learner a text would be written over their words and read aloud — the page is a button, and then the lines. The English names inside the code (`takk/story.py`, `POST /api/story`) are not learner-facing and stay.

    Each request draws its words afresh from `known`, so the second six lines lean on whatever the first six left weakest. The model sees only the words of the request it is writing, so a story is continuous in the reading rather than in the telling — whether that shows is the first thing to look at in a browser.

14. **Tecken as charts** (`frontend/src/routes/tecken/+page.svelte`): a bar chart of the vocabulary over the schedule and a line chart of signs met over time, then "Nollställ", and nothing else. The bars are one per state, box 0 included (user, 2026-09-21) — nya, imorgon, 3 dagar, 7 dagar, 21 dagar — with the count written over each. The line is the cumulative number of signs with a `first` date, which is what "met" means: a sign picked and not yet practised has no such day and is not counted. Its axis runs from the earliest of those days to now, with both ends written out ("3 sep." and "idag", user 2026-09-21) — unlabelled, a week and a year looked the same. Both are read from the existing store, so no history is written, and both are inline SVG — one bar series and one line, each a single series, so there is no categorical palette to get wrong and no charting dependency to carry. The bars use the page's accent and the line its "right" green, the axes are a single recessive rule, and the counts are written directly rather than hovered for. — done, checked with `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`; the geometry was checked by arithmetic rather than by looking at it, since the machine is headless. Not seen in a browser yet.

    **The table of every practised sign is gone**, with the counts per box it sat under: the map has a box for the charts and the reset and none for a table. It was where the spaced repetition was inspected while it was being built, so it is the one thing to bring back — behind a fold — if reading the boxes by hand turns out to be needed again.

15. **Later**, in the order they are likely to matter: a **conversation with an LLM** the learner answers by speaking and signing, corrected turn by turn, with the missed signs brought back in later turns, which is spaced repetition inside a conversation (user, 2026-09-20); **free signing**, where the learner speaks and signs whatever they like and the app reports which key words were signed — the speech model is a CTC model, so decoding its emissions gives the transcript and the content words of it that exist in the lexicon become the expected signs, after which the existing alignment and scoring run unchanged; and **state on the server**, accounts and a database, when the browser's own storage actually gets in the way (a second device, or more than one learner) and not before. — planned

## Decisions

- **The camera says how the signer sits, not how fast it runs** (user, 2026-09-21). The page carries the framing feedback of step 11 and nothing about frames per second.

- **The blueprint is the layout, and the pages stay very minimal** (user, 2026-09-21). Each screen holds what its box in the map holds, in that order, and nothing beside it, and it shows the thing rather than explaining it: no page tells the learner how the mode they are in works. No icons, no cards or shadows, no colour beyond what marks a state, no UI framework or component library, and no headings or helper text the map has no room for. Charts are the exception (user, 2026-09-21): a charting library is fine where it makes the graphs much better. Where the map labels something in Swedish ("Sök efter ord eller teman", "Lägg till alla", "Nollställ"), that wording is the label.

- **The learner chooses the vocabulary by searching the lexicon** (user, 2026-09-21). Ready-made lists — the starter packs and the lexicon's categories as a picker — are dropped in favour of search, by word or by theme or by signing. The categories remain underneath as what a theme search matches, which is what they were always good for: they are subject areas rather than a learning order (see findings), so they made a poor picker and make a reasonable search index.

- **The app is in Swedish, and keeps the learner's state in the browser until something needs more** (user, 2026-09-20). No accounts and no database for as long as this holds. The LLM steps are also the point of the exercise and not only a means, as the user wants the app to be an occasion to use LLM APIs.

- **The app is two modes and nothing else** (user, 2026-09-21): new words, and repetition in the story. A word is taught with its clip the first time and recalled from memory afterwards; a card says only whether the sign was right; nothing is pressed between cards.

- **Everything is spoken** (user, 2026-09-21). TAKK is signed while speaking, so every attempt is said aloud however few of its words are signed, and the microphone is required throughout — including a word being taught for the first time. The rule is uniform because a learner should not have to remember which kind of turn they are in, which is worth more than the two arguments for exempting the teaching turn (a clip is copied rather than recalled, and whole-recording scoring is more forgiving than alignment). The one exception is Sök med tecken, which is a lookup and not an attempt.

- **A pass always demotes, but promotes only a sign that was due** (user, 2026-09-21). A box is a claim about an interval — the third means "still remembered after seven days" — and an answer on the second day has not tested that, so promoting early would push the next repetition out to an interval that was never met, and a word could climb out of the boxes by being drilled in one sitting; it is why Anki does not reschedule "review ahead" by default. A miss is evidence whatever the day, since forgetting a sign that was not due means its interval was already too long. The asymmetry means an extra pass can only repair the boxes, never inflate them.

- **The model picks which of a turn's words to use, and uses them uninflected** (user, 2026-09-21). The words are offered with only the first required, since deciding the combination in the app produced sentences that listed words instead of saying something (see findings). No inflection: the signs are found in the recording by timing those words in the audio, so a form that was not said as written scores below `MIN_WORD_SCORE` and is refused, and forbidding inflection also makes the whole answer checkable (every word a whole token, once) instead of trusting the model to report which form it chose. Swedish gives this up cheaply, since the infinitive is the dictionary form ("vill äta") and a noun sits in its base form behind an article ("en hund"). If the texts turn out stilted, the model returning `{word, spoken}` pairs is the change to make. A text that breaks the contract is asked for again once, with what was wrong.

- **A learner's choices never go in the URL** (user, 2026-09-21): what is being practised is chosen in the page, before a pass starts.

- **The frontend is a SvelteKit app, built and hosted apart from the API** (user, 2026-09-20: a real learning app is coming, with lesson flow, progress and phone use, and a Node build step is acceptable). `frontend/` holds it (SvelteKit 2, Svelte 5, TypeScript, Vite, `adapter-static`, prerendered with `ssr = false`), outside the Python package so that `node_modules` and the build never reach the wheel. The API does not serve it: Vite's dev and preview servers proxy `/api` to `127.0.0.1:8002`, so only one port is forwarded from this machine, and a deployed build reaches the API through `VITE_API_BASE`. The user rejected serving the built files from FastAPI as coupling that hurts deployment and availability.

- **The large unchanging files the browser downloads ("media") are meant for a static host, not the API process**: `holistic_landmarker.task` (13.7 MB, `/api/model`) and the lexicon clips (17 GB in `data/raw/sts-lexikon/movies`, `/api/reference/<clip id>`). They are read off disk, while `POST /api/attempt` is what needs torch, the checkpoint and the ~45 s startup. Nothing moves yet; the design rule is that the API hands the frontend URLs for clips and the model rather than paths it assembles itself, so they can be repointed at a CDN server-side. The goal is that searching the lexicon and watching clips keep working while the scoring API is asleep — which step 9 makes more important, since search is now how vocabulary grows.

- **The app applies the threshold, it does not choose it.** What an attempt has to reach is decided from the model's numbers, in the decisions of ROADMAP.md (a provisional 0.38, `--threshold`).

- **Judging a sentence sign by sign while it is signed was built and then removed** (user, 2026-09-20). It worked: a verdict appeared about one sign behind the signer, from a rest or from the next key word being spoken. The commits are in the history (reverted in `a0d3e0a` and the three before it) if it is ever wanted again.

## Findings

- **The word a learner practises is not the name of the sign that scores it**, and every place that forgets it breaks (2026-09-20 and 2026-09-21). Entries of one sign form are one class labelled by its lowest entry, so "äta" is scored as `sts:livsmedel-01265`, "blå" as `sts:öga-02636` and "grön" as `sts:land-00416`. The flash cards, the progress page and the story each had to be fixed for it separately: a story once had the model write about "öga" while the learner was practising "blå", the sentence passed validation, nothing was marked, the alignment listened for the wrong word and the verdict was "Inga händer syntes". The word, the lexicon entry and the sign are kept apart everywhere, and `POST /api/attempt` takes the learner's own `spoken` word per sign.

- **JavaScript's `\b` is ASCII-only, so Swedish words were left unmarked** (user, 2026-09-21, found on their first story). "å" is not a word character, so `\bblå\b` matches nothing at all and the page printed the part without marking blå while the server counted, scored and recorded it. Any word ending or beginning in å, ä or ö was affected. The split lives in `frontend/src/lib/text.ts` with the boundary written out as `[\p{L}\p{N}_]` lookarounds, and is unit tested on exactly these words.

- **Forced alignment never fails, so a sentence signed in silence was scored as if it had been spoken** (user, 2026-09-21). `forced_align` is a Viterbi path: it returns an alignment for any audio, and the wildcard token makes one cheap to find, so silence gives each key word a narrow span somewhere and the split cuts between them. The user signed two signs without speaking and got two verdicts, both accepted. The alignment's own score tells the cases apart: against silence or room noise the words score -5.4 to -3.9 (measured with "hej" and "mamma"), while a word actually spoken scores near 0. `MIN_WORD_SCORE` is -3.0, between the two. The threshold is provisional in the same way as the acceptance threshold of ROADMAP.md — mumbling and a noisy room have not been measured — so the server prints the scores of every refused sentence for as long as that holds.

- **Splitting at the rests could not tell a skipped sign from a failed one**, which is why the speech split is now the only one (user, 2026-09-21). The rest split (`split_signs`, runs of frames with a raised hand at least 0.4 s apart) answered a skipped sign by voiding the whole sentence, losing the verdicts on the signs that were right, while the speech split cuts by the word's place in the audio and makes a skipped sign an ordinary miss. Keeping both would have meant an "unless the rest split ran" clause in every rule of the repetition schedule, since a miss and a void have opposite effects on a box. It measured well on the 51 Swedish browser recordings — every one of the 1,275 pairs joined end to end split into exactly two, with the hands lost for at most 0.23 s inside a sign against at least 0.43 s between two — so it was accuracy that was never the problem.

- **A word could reach the third box in a single pass** (user, 2026-09-21). A sign was put back in the queue whenever it was in the first box after an answer, which was meant to catch a missed sign but also caught a *new* word answered correctly, since a first accept moves a word from no box to box 1; and the record button stayed enabled after a verdict, so recording the same card again counted as a second answer. The rule that replaced it: the box moves once per pass, and a second recording of the same card is a retry that shows a verdict and changes nothing.

- **Imposing the combination made the sentences worse than offering it** (user, 2026-09-21). Asked for a sentence containing both "röd" and "blå", the model wrote "Nallen har en röd napp och ett öga." — two objects invented to carry the two colours, which is a list with grammar rather than a sentence about anything. The words are now offered with only the head required, so the same pair can produce "Har du en röd tröja?" and blå waits for a turn it fits. Reading the set off the text makes the validation smaller rather than larger: the head must be there, no word twice, at most three in all.

- **What a theme should offer first is `lexicon_hits`, and two traps sit in the lexicon's words** (2026-09-20). Of the three counts the entry pages publish, `lexicon_hits` is on every entry and its head is what TAKK teaches (Djur: hund, katt, djur; Kläder: kläder, sko, tröja; Sport: träna, fotboll, ishockey). `corpus_hits` exists for only 6,003 of 21,692 entries, and `survey_hits` is close to an anti-signal, since the surveys ask about rare signs and put avocado and hamster first. The first trap is the word-versus-sign-name one above: ordered naively, "Djur" began at "hane" and "batteri". The second is Österberg's 1916 dictionary, 417 entries of historical sign forms in a category of their own, which must never be taught — "hjälp" resolved into one of them until a heading was made to beat another entry's `also` wording. The lexicon's 14,343 category memberships come out as 9,887 words.

- **The lexicon's categories are subject areas, not a learning order** (2026-09-20). By size they are Sport 2,238 signs, Geografi 1,524, Sjukvård 1,136, Egennamn 757, and only then Barn 320, Kläder 131, Känslor 92, Familj 74, Hälsningsfras 14, so a beginner cannot start from them — which is what the starter packs existed for, and why search replaced both. What a beginner needs is covered, though: 38 of 39 words of a typical TAKK starter vocabulary resolve to a lexicon sign (only "godnatt" is missing). The categories are read off the entry pages by `parse_entry`, which takes the whole page in one pass (user, 2026-09-20: download everything about the dataset once, rather than adding a second file per field), so it also reads the entry's other wording, its English translation and its hit counts.

- **Timing the spoken words costs one model and half a second** (`takk/speech.py`). Forced alignment needs the words, not a transcript, which is exactly what a TAKK sentence already is. KBLab's `easyaligner` (https://github.com/kb-labb/easyaligner, MIT) is the same recipe packaged but was not adopted: it pins `torch>=2.7,<2.9` against this project's 2.14 and is built for batch alignment over directories, with `pyannote-audio` and `silero-vad` that its own documentation says alignment does not use. Only `torchaudio.functional.forced_align` is used here, plus the wildcard applied between the words rather than only at the ends. A wildcard column of zeros is the highest possible log probability, so each key word gets close to the fewest frames it can and comes out as a narrow span at its clearest frames, which is enough to cut between them. `KBLab/wav2vec2-large-voxrex-swedish` is 1.2 GB, loads in 2.8 s and aligns a 4 s recording in 0.4 s on the CPU.

- **The app end to end** (headless Chrome with the `sju` recording as a fake webcam, `--use-file-for-fake-video-capture`, a 2.9 s recording each): signing sju for sju is accepted with score 0.80; the same attempt for tupp scores 0.05 and for klassifikator 0.27, both rejected. Extraction without a seek per frame takes about 7 s for 2.9 s of video, so the answer comes about 7 s after the recording stops. Live tracking in that browser reaches only 2–6 fps (WebGL on SwiftShader), so it shows that the page works, not how fast it is on a real GPU; even at 2 fps an attempt of sju was accepted (0.81) and the same video for tupp rejected (0.06).
